# Science (DLP) · Year 3 SK · Unit 6 Measurement

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U06-29 · Recognise area and volume units

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.1
**References** · Textbook pp. 80, 85 · Activity Book pp. 53, 58
**Learning objective** · Select cm², cm³, mL or L for four examples and explain the choice for one example.
**Materials** · Unit cards, a 1 cm grid square, a cube and labelled containers.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 4 min | Hold up a paper square and a small empty container. | Identify which example is a surface and which can hold something. |
| Main activity · 13 min | Model using cm² for a small surface and cm³ for space taken up by a cube. Compare container markings in mL and L. | Match the four unit cards with a postage stamp, a wooden cube, a spoonful and a bottle. |
| Practice and assessment · 10 min | Give four new examples and a word bank. Check each unit and one explanation. | Select cm², cm³, mL or L for each example and justify one choice. |
| Closure · 3 min | Ask which unit helps describe the area of a playing field. | Suggest m² and explain why a larger unit is suitable. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U06-30 · Measure a regular area

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.2
**References** · Textbook pp. 81–82 · Activity Book pp. 54–55
**Learning objective** · Cover a regular outline with 1 cm squares and give the total in cm².
**Materials** · 1 cm square tiles, rectangular outlines and centimetre grids.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show two rectangular cards and ask how to compare the covered space. | Suggest counting equal squares. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model covering an outline without gaps or overlaps. Check that a 3-by-4 rectangle has 12 unit squares. | Cover two outlines and compare counting individual rows with counting all the tiles. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give each pupil a 4-by-5 rectangle. Check full coverage and the unit in the answer. | Cover the rectangle with 1 cm squares and record 20 cm². |
| Closure · 5 min | Move two tiles so they overlap. | Explain why that arrangement gives an unreliable count. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U06-31 · Estimate an irregular area

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.3
**References** · Textbook pp. 83–84 · Activity Book pp. 56
**Learning objective** · Estimate the area of a leaf outline on a centimetre grid and explain how partial squares were counted.
**Materials** · Two leaf outlines on 1 cm grids and coloured pencils.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a leaf outline with square boundaries crossing its edge. | Identify full and partial squares. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Demonstrate counting full squares, then including partial squares that are half-full or more. State the rule before counting. | Estimate one outline in pairs and compare borderline squares using the agreed rule. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a fresh gridded outline. Check the count of full and eligible partial squares and consistent use of the agreed rule. | Record an estimate with cm² and state how partial squares were counted. |
| Closure · 5 min | Show two close estimates for the same leaf. | Explain why judgement about edge squares can differ. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U06-32 · Measure the volume of a box

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.4
**References** · Textbook pp. 86–87 · Activity Book pp. 57–58
**Learning objective** · Fill a small rectangular container with 1 cm cubes and record its volume in cm³.
**Materials** · 1 cm cubes, a 3-by-2-by-2 cube box and recording sheets.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Display a shallow layer of cubes beside an empty box. | Predict how many identical layers will fit. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Build the first layer and demonstrate making a second layer without gaps. Relate each cube to 1 cm³. | Fill boxes, sketch layers and count cubes per layer before finding the total. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give each pupil a box that fits 3 by 2 by 2 cubes. Check placement, count and unit. | Fill the box and report 12 cm³, using two layers of six cubes. |
| Closure · 5 min | Point to an empty corner left between cubes. | Explain how that gap affects the measured volume. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U06-33 · Measure a liquid volume

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.5
**References** · Textbook pp. 88–90 · Activity Book pp. 59–60
**Learning objective** · Choose a suitable measuring cylinder and record two readings at eye level in mL.
**Materials** · Plastic measuring cylinders, water, trays and a stable marked shelf.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a cylinder on a bench and ask how two pupils can get the same reading. | Suggest standing in the right position and reading the same mark. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Demonstrate choosing a suitable cylinder, placing it flat and reading the bottom of the water meniscus at eye level. | Pour sample amounts, check the scale interval and record two readings with a partner. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Prepare two cylinders at marked levels. Check each pupil's choice of tool, viewpoint and both readings. | Read each cylinder independently and write the values in mL. |
| Closure · 5 min | Show a reading taken from above. | Adjust viewpoint to eye level and identify the bottom of the meniscus. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U06-34 · Calculate displaced volume

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.6
**References** · Textbook pp. 91–92 · Activity Book pp. 61–62
**Learning objective** · Record the initial and final water levels for a submerged object and calculate the difference.
**Materials** · Measuring cylinders, water, thread, small sinking stones and towels.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Hold up an irregular stone and a cube. | Explain which item cannot be filled or measured readily with cubes. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Record the starting water level. Lower the stone fully with thread without splashing and read the new level. | Observe both readings, calculate the difference and repeat with a second stone. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give each pupil figures of 35 mL before and 52 mL after. Check the subtraction and use of a volume unit. | Calculate 17 mL of displaced water and explain why the stone's volume is 17 cm³. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what happens if part of the stone remains above water. | Explain that only the submerged part displaces water. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U06-35 · Explain an area or volume method

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.7
**References** · Textbook pp. 81–84, 88–94 · Activity Book pp. 54–56, 59–62
**Learning objective** · Present two measurements with units and justify one tool or technique used.
**Materials** · Station cards, rulers, grid sheets, cylinder readings and report slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a blank measurement card with a number but no units. | Suggest which detail is needed to interpret the number. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Set up one area and one cylinder-reading station. Model recording an estimate or reading so another group can reproduce it. | Take both measurements, identify the technique used at each station and exchange results. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Check each pupil's two recorded measurements and one explanation that connects a tool or method to the task. | Submit a mini-report with one area in cm², one volume in mL and a justified choice of technique. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which technique is needed to read water levels accurately. | Describe the level viewpoint and meniscus. |
