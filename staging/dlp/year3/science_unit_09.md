# Science (DLP) · Year 3 SK · Unit 9 The Solar System

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U09-44 · Identify Solar System members

**Standards** · CS 9.1 · LS 9.1.1
**References** · Textbook pp. 116–119 · Activity Book pp. 77–79
**Learning objective** · Label the Sun and the eight planets in order and name two other types of Solar System objects.
**Materials** · Sun and planet cards, an orbit strip and pictures of other Solar System objects.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 4 min | Show the Sun card and ask where it belongs in the model. | Place it in the centre of the orbit model. |
| Main activity · 13 min | Guide pairs to order the eight planets outward from the Sun. Show examples of a moon, asteroid and comet. | Arrange planet cards and group the other examples by object type. |
| Practice and assessment · 10 min | Check each pupil's sequence and names for any two additional types, such as a natural satellite and a comet. | Label the Sun and eight planets in order and name two other types of object. |
| Closure · 3 min | Ask pupils to name the planet immediately outside Earth's orbit. | Name Mars using the sequence. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U09-45 · Compare planet temperatures

**Standards** · CS 9.1 · LS 9.1.2
**References** · Textbook pp. 120–121 · Activity Book pp. 80–81
**Learning objective** · Use a supplied data chart to state the general trend with distance and explain why Venus is an exception.
**Materials** · Planet distance and temperature chart, graph paper and comparison slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show the positions of Mercury and Venus and ask which pupils think is hotter. | Predict and explain the reason. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Guide pupils to plot or order the temperature data. Explain that the general trend includes exceptions, with Venus hotter than Mercury because its atmosphere traps heat. | Compare temperatures across the sequence and mark Venus as an exception. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Check each pupil's statement of the trend and explanation of Venus using the supplied chart and atmosphere note. | Write the general trend and cite Mercury and Venus as a comparison that supports the exception. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask whether distance alone predicts every temperature. | Explain what the Venus example adds. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U09-46 · Model orbits around the Sun

**Standards** · CS 9.1 · LS 9.1.3
**References** · Textbook pp. 122–123 · Activity Book pp. 78, 82
**Learning objective** · Demonstrate an orbit and distinguish a planet's revolution from its rotation.
**Materials** · Sun marker, floor orbit ring, toy planet with a marked spot and diagrams.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a spinning object and ask what moves around its centre. | Describe a spin in place. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model a marked planet rotating while it travels around the Sun marker. Guide pairs to draw arrows for the two motions. | Move the planet through one orbit while it spins and label both motions on a sketch. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Watch each pupil demonstrate a complete orbit and check the direction and placement of the separate rotation arrow. | Show one revolution and explain how it differs from a spin about the planet's own axis. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which motion takes Earth about one year. | Identify its revolution around the Sun. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U09-47 · Compare revolution times

**Standards** · CS 9.1 · LS 9.1.4
**References** · Textbook pp. 124 · Activity Book pp. 83
**Learning objective** · Order four planets by their period of revolution and relate the pattern to distance from the Sun.
**Materials** · Four planet-distance cards, orbital-period data and number lines.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Ask whether the same number of Earth days means the same fraction of a year on all planets. | Suggest that orbital periods can differ. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use a single unit on the data chart. Guide comparisons of Mercury, Earth, Mars and Jupiter by position and period. | Place the four cards on two strips, one by distance and one by orbital period, and compare their order. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Check each pupil's four-planet order and one supporting comparison with data. | Order the periods from shortest to longest and explain the link with distance from the Sun. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which planet from the set has a longer orbital period than Mars. | Name Jupiter and cite the chart. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U09-48 · Communicate Solar System findings

**Standards** · CS 9.1 · LS 9.1.5
**References** · Textbook pp. 121–126 · Activity Book pp. 84
**Learning objective** · Use a diagram and a data point to explain one relationship in the Solar System.
**Materials** · Planet data tables, labelled orbit sketches and mini-poster sheets.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a blank orbit diagram and ask what labels would make it readable. | Propose the Sun, orbit and planet labels. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Offer a choice of explaining distance and orbital period or distance and temperature. Show how to use one data point beside the labelled diagram. | Choose a relationship, make a diagram and rehearse an evidence-based explanation. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Check each pupil's diagram, the relationship stated and a relevant data point. Check that a temperature explanation recognises the exception if selected. | Present the diagram and use the data to support one accurate conclusion. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask pupils to point to the evidence for their conclusion. | Identify the data point and the relationship it supports. |
