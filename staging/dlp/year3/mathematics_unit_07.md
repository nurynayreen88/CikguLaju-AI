# Mathematics (DLP) · Year 3 SK · Unit 7 Shapes

## DLP-MAT-Y3-U07-62 · Identify and describe prisms

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.1, 7.1.2
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 85–87 · Activity Book pp. 129–131
**Learning objective** · Name three prism models and label the bases, faces, edges and vertices on one sketch.
**Materials** · Square, rectangular and triangular prism models, labels and sketch paper.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a triangular prism end-on, then turn it sideways. | Identify the triangular end and describe what stays the same as the model is turned. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Name prisms using their two matching parallel bases. Trace edges and mark vertices on each model. Use the school textbook's square-prism and rectangular-prism names. | Sort three models by their base shapes and label one sketch with bases, faces, an edge and a vertex. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show the three models and give a triangular-prism sketch. Check three names and identification of its two triangular bases, five faces, nine edges and six vertices. | Name the three models. Mark both triangular bases and label one other face, one edge and one vertex. |
| Closure · 5 min | Turn a prism so its base is not facing down. | Point to the matching pair of bases and state its name. |

## DLP-MAT-Y3-U07-63 · Compare prisms and non-prisms

**Standards** · CS 7.2 · LS 7.2.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 85–88 · Activity Book pp. 129–131
**Learning objective** · Use two models to compare shape and number of bases, surfaces, vertices and edges.
**Materials** · Triangular prism and square pyramid models, counting flags and comparison grids.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 10 min | Show the prism and pyramid from the side. | Identify a feature each shares and a difference in shape. |
| Main activity · 45 min | Trace both models, identify their bases and show how matching parallel bases distinguish the prism. Count each feature using removable flags. | Compare the number and shape of bases, faces, edges and vertices without double-counting. |
| Practice and assessment · 25 min | Give a triangular prism and a square pyramid. Check prism 2 bases, 5 faces, 9 edges and 6 vertices, and pyramid 1 base, 5 faces, 8 edges and 5 vertices. | Complete the grid. Explain how the shape and number of bases distinguish the prism from the pyramid. |
| Closure · 10 min | Ask which model has two equal triangular bases. | Identify the prism and point to both bases. |

## DLP-MAT-Y3-U07-64 · Recognise regular polygons and make patterns

**Standards** · CS 7.3 · LS 7.3.1, 7.3.2
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 89–91 · Activity Book pp. 132–134
**Learning objective** · Name four regular polygons and construct a repeating pattern with a stated rule.
**Materials** · Regular and irregular polygon cards, rulers and coloured shape tiles.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a square and a long rectangle. | Describe which has sides all the same length and angles all the same size. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use regular triangle, square, pentagon and hexagon cards. Check equal sides and angles, then model a repeating motif with two shapes. | Name the polygons, compare them to irregular examples and build a repeat with at least three copies of its motif. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give cards for an equilateral triangle, square, regular pentagon and regular hexagon. Check all four names and three valid repeats of a chosen motif. | Name the shapes and make a pattern, state its rule and show the next shape. |
| Closure · 5 min | Cover one tile in a repeated group. | Predict the missing shape by tracing the rule. |

## DLP-MAT-Y3-U07-65 · Find axes of symmetry

**Standards** · CS 7.4 · LS 7.4.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 92–93 · Activity Book pp. 135
**Learning objective** · Use folding or marking to draw the axes of symmetry on three diagrams.
**Materials** · Paper square, non-square rectangle, non-equilateral isosceles triangle, scissors and mirrors.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Hold a folded rectangle with matching corners. | Show what it means for the two halves to coincide. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model folding and checking more than one possible line. Use the mirror to verify why a diagonal of a non-square rectangle fails. | Test candidate lines, trace folds that overlap and record the total for each shape. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a square, a non-square rectangle and a non-equilateral isosceles triangle. Check four, two and one axes respectively. | Draw every axis on each diagram and use a fold or reflection to justify one choice. |
| Closure · 5 min | Show a line that divides a rectangle into equal areas but fails the fold test. | Explain why equal area alone does not establish line symmetry. |

## DLP-MAT-Y3-U07-66 · Solve shape and symmetry problems

**Standards** · CS 7.5 · LS 7.5.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 94–95 · Activity Book pp. 136
**Learning objective** · Use a labelled model or drawing to justify a choice about a prism or a line of symmetry.
**Materials** · Parcel models, grid drawings, straightedges and mirrors.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a tent-shaped box and ask which two ends can match. | Identify the triangular ends and explain a way to check them. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Compare a prism-shaped parcel with a pyramid, then use a mirror line on a separate design. Ask pairs to justify their choices with labelled evidence. | Choose a model using the bases and test a candidate symmetry line on the drawing. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask which of a triangular prism or square pyramid can provide two matching triangular end panels. Also ask pupils to mark one valid axis on a non-square rectangular label. | Choose the triangular prism, label both bases and mark a horizontal or vertical axis on the label. Explain both choices. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what observation would disprove a claimed axis. | Describe where a reflected point or edge fails to land on its matching part. |

## DLP-MAT-Y3-R07 · Review: Shapes

**Standards** · CS 7.1, 7.2, 7.4 · Review LS 7.1.1, 7.2.1, 7.4.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 96 · Activity Book pp. 137–138
**Learning objective** · Sort solids and mark and justify a line of symmetry on a new diagram.
**Materials** · Solid cards, folding paper and a short comparison chart.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a solid that has one triangular face visible. | Ask what other faces must be inspected to identify the solid. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Review identifying matching parallel bases and checking a symmetry line by folding. Let pairs sort new solid cards. | Group prisms and non-prisms and test one axis on a cut-out. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a rectangular prism, a triangular prism and a square pyramid plus a non-square rectangle. Check the first two in the prism group and accept either valid axis on the rectangle. | Record the groups, mark a valid axis and justify using matching features or halves. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask for one property shared by the two prism cards. | State that both have a matching pair of parallel bases. |
