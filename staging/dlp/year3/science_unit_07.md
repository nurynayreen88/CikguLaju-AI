# Science (DLP) · Year 3 SK · Unit 7 Density

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U07-36 · Investigate floating and sinking

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.1
**References** · Textbook pp. 96–97 · Activity Book pp. 63
**Learning objective** · Record the behaviour of four items in water and propose one explanation based on the observations.
**Materials** · Water trays, metal washer, solid plastic block, cork and a small stone.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Hold up a cork and washer. Ask what pupils expect each to do in water. | Predict one result and give a reason. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model lowering each item gently without pushing it under. Supervise tests with four items and ask which observations can support an explanation. | Test each item, note where it comes to rest and distinguish observations from explanations. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Check each pupil's four entries and one plausible explanation grounded in the trial. | Record whether each item floats or sinks and use one result to support an inference. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask whether being small guarantees that an item floats. | Use the washer or stone as evidence. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U07-37 · Compare density with water

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.2
**References** · Textbook pp. 98–99 · Activity Book pp. 64–66
**Learning objective** · Use fresh-water observations to infer which tested items are denser or less dense than water.
**Materials** · Water, transparent tubs, wood blocks, washers and a results table.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a small washer and a larger piece of solid wood. | Use last lesson's observations to predict their behaviour. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Repeat the tests and introduce denser and less dense than water for the specimens. Discuss why size alone does not settle the comparison. | Sort the tested solid objects by relative density and explain one case to a partner. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give each pupil results for a floating cork and a sinking solid bead. Check both comparisons with water. | Classify each tested item as denser or less dense than water and cite its behaviour. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which comparison belongs beside an object that stays at the surface. | Give the density relationship for the specimen. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U07-38 · Make water denser

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.3
**References** · Textbook pp. 99–101 · Activity Book pp. 68
**Learning objective** · Compare an object's behaviour in fresh and salt water and explain the change using density.
**Materials** · Clear cups, water, table salt, stirrers and an egg with a checked starting result.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show an egg that sinks in fresh water and ask how the water could be changed. | Suggest dissolving salt and predict the outcome. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Set up equal cups of fresh and pretested salt water. Move the same egg gently between cups while keeping other conditions constant. | Observe each trial and describe what changed about the liquid. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Check each pupil's comparison diagram for both observations and the explanation that dissolved salt increases the liquid's density. | Show where the egg rests in both cups and use density to explain why its position changes. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what stayed the same in both trials. | Name the same egg and explain why it was reused. |

## DLP-SCI-Y3-U07-39 · Explain a use of density

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.4
**References** · Textbook pp. 102–104 · Activity Book pp. 67–68
**Learning objective** · Use a labelled drawing and one classroom observation to explain why the same object floats higher in denser salt water.
**Materials** · Fresh- and salt-water trial records, a photo of a salt lake and drawing sheets.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a picture of a person floating high in a salt lake. | Recall what changed when salt was added in class. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Compare the class observations with the lake example. Model labelled sketches showing the same object in the two liquids. | Draw a comparison and use one result from the trial to explain the application. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Check each pupil's drawing for both liquid labels, one accurate observation and the link to density. | Submit a labelled drawing and explain why the object floats higher in denser salt water. |
| Closure · 5 min | Invite pupils to point to the label that explains the difference. | Point to the denser liquid and explain its connection to the observed position. |
