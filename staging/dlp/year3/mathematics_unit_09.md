# Mathematics (DLP) · Year 3 SK · Unit 9 Data

## DLP-MAT-Y3-U09-70 · Collect, classify and sort data

**Standards** · CS 9.1 · LS 9.1.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 107–108 · Activity Book pp. 147
**Learning objective** · Gather responses, group them into categories and check tally totals against a count.
**Materials** · Anonymous preference cards, four category labels and tally sheets.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show five response cards and ask how to count them without losing any. | Suggest sorting by category and checking the original total. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Collect one response per pupil and demonstrate grouping tallies in fives. Ask groups to compare the tally total with the number of cards. | Sort one response to one category, tally each and check the totals against the pile count. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Provide 12 cards with 5 reading, 4 drawing and 3 puzzles. Check a correctly labelled tally table, three frequencies and total 12. | Independently classify and tally all 12 and show how the result was checked. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what to check when frequencies add to 13. | Look for a card counted twice or a tally mark entered twice. |

## DLP-MAT-Y3-U09-71 · Read a pie chart

**Standards** · CS 9.2 · LS 9.2.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 109 · Activity Book pp. 148
**Learning objective** · Use the title, key and labelled values to answer three questions about categories and frequencies.
**Materials** · Teacher-prepared labelled pie charts and question cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a chart title and colour key without the slices. | Predict what categories the chart will display. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Display a pie chart for 20 votes, with red 8, blue 6, green 4 and yellow 2, using proportional sectors. Trace from the key to a labelled slice before reading its count. | Use the title and legend to match slices and compare counts. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a labelled pie chart for 16 votes: reading 8, drawing 4, puzzles 3 and music 1. Ask the largest category, number for puzzles and difference between drawing and music. Check reading, 3 and 3. | Answer all three and point to the slice and key used for one. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why a larger slice usually represents more votes on the same chart. | Explain the connection between slice size and count. |

## DLP-MAT-Y3-U09-72 · Compare three displays of the same data

**Standards** · CS 9.3 · LS 9.3.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 110–112 · Activity Book pp. 149–150
**Learning objective** · Match a pictograph, a bar chart and a pie chart and verify a frequency in all three displays.
**Materials** · A pictograph, a bar chart, a pie chart and a shared data table.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a pictograph key with one symbol for two pupils. | Read three symbols as six pupils. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Compare displays for 8 reading, 6 drawing and 4 puzzles. Match the pictograph key and the bar scale to the labelled pie sectors. | Read the same category in all three forms and explain how each display encodes its value. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give three displays of 6 reading, 4 drawing and 2 puzzles. Check that drawing is two pictograph symbols, bar height four and a pie sector labelled four. | Match each display to the table and verify the drawing frequency across all three. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why a pictograph with two symbols can represent four pupils. | Refer to the key and calculate 2 × 2. |

## DLP-MAT-Y3-U09-73 · Solve data handling problems

**Standards** · CS 9.4 · LS 9.4.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 113–115 · Activity Book pp. 151–152
**Learning objective** · Answer two contextual questions and support one decision using data from a chart.
**Materials** · A class-preference chart, a school-club scenario and answer slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a chart of pupils' chosen clubs and ask what a teacher could decide from it. | Suggest a decision and identify which category counts are needed. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model reading a bar and using differences to make a recommendation. Let pairs compare two categories and explain their evidence. | Find counts and differences, then connect a practical decision to the data. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 12 sports, 9 art, 6 music and 3 chess votes. Ask how many more votes sports received than music and the total for art and chess. Check 6 and 12. Check that sports and art are chosen and justified with 12 and 9 votes. | Calculate the difference and total requested. Name two clubs to schedule first and cite their vote counts. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what would change if another class were added. | Explain that the combined counts might change the ranking. |

## DLP-MAT-Y3-R09 · Review: Data

**Standards** · CS 9.3, 9.4 · Review LS 9.3.1, 9.4.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 116 · Activity Book pp. 153–154
**Learning objective** · Compare the same data in two displays and use the frequencies to justify one statement.
**Materials** · A tally table, pictograph, bar chart and short review slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a tally of five and one pictograph key. | Read the same count in two representations. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use a shared set of category counts to match two displays. Discuss how titles, keys and scales help detect an error. | Compare two displays and find a statement supported by both. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a bar chart and pictograph for 6 walking, 4 bus and 2 bicycle responses with a two-pupil key. Check that walking exceeds bus by 2. | Use the bar values and pictograph key to justify the comparison. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why the bar scale and pictograph key matter. | Explain how each helps turn a picture into a count. |
