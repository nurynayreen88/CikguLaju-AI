# Mathematics (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 8 Data

Four 60-minute RPH for CS 8.1–8.3. The bar-chart standard is taught in two lessons, one for each orientation. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-8-source-check).

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U08-01 · Collect and Organise Everyday Data

**Standards** · CS 8.1 · LS 8.1.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 91–93 · Activity Book pp. 149–150
**Learning objective** · Collect one response from each pupil in a small group, record each response once and check that the category totals match the number of responses.
**Materials** · Three activity picture cards, response tokens, tally sheets and an individual set of eight coded survey slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Ask which of three indoor activities the class would choose. Discuss how to give everyone one vote. | Suggest recording one choice for each pupil. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model putting one tally in the correct category for each response and bundling every fifth mark. Assign a collector and checker in each group, then swap roles. | Collect responses, sort tokens into categories, tally the choices and check that the counts add up to the number surveyed. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Hand out eight anonymous survey slips with four drawing choices, three reading choices and one puzzle choice in mixed order. Check each tally and total. | Independently sort and tally the slips, write the three counts and check that 4 + 3 + 1 = 8. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what a total of nine would suggest if only eight slips were collected. | Explain that a response may have been counted twice or an extra mark may have been added. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils recorded all categories and checked the total correctly. Practise moving each counted slip to a separate pile with pupils who counted a slip twice.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U08-02 · Read a Vertical Bar Chart

**Standards** · CS 8.2 · LS 8.2.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 94–96 · Activity Book p. 151 · Activity 1
**Learning objective** · Identify the meaning of both axes, read three bar values and identify the highest and lowest categories on a vertical bar chart.
**Materials** · Teacher-drawn vertical charts with one-unit marks, axis labels, rulers and individual answer slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Display a chart titled Books Borrowed and ask what its title tells us before reading the bars. | Explain what is counted and locate the category names. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Show how to move from a category to the top of its bar and across to the number axis. Point out the zero and one-unit graduations. | Take turns tracing bar tops to the scale, stating values with their units and finding the highest and lowest bars. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Display a new chart showing four adventure books, seven animal books and two poetry books borrowed. Ask for both axes, all three values and the two extremes. | Independently name the category and number axes, record 4, 7 and 2, and identify animal books as highest and poetry books as lowest. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why the scale matters even if a bar looks tall. | Explain that its height must be read against the numbered marks to obtain a count. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils identified both axes, read all three values and found both extremes. Trace across from the bar top with pupils who read between adjacent ticks.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U08-03 · Read a Horizontal Bar Chart

**Standards** · CS 8.2 · LS 8.2.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 94–96 · Activity Book p. 152 · Activity 2
**Learning objective** · Read four values from a horizontal bar chart, identify the largest category and calculate the total of the four values.
**Materials** · Teacher-drawn horizontal charts with zero and one-unit graduations, category cards, rulers and answer slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Display a horizontal chart and ask which axis carries the numbers. | Point to the horizontal number axis and the vertical category axis. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model following a bar to its right end and then down to the scale. Have pairs read a chart and check their values by tracing the same path. | Read values with category names, compare the bar lengths and add the counts when asked for the total. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show a chart of six bus riders, five walkers, two cyclists and four car riders. Ask for all counts, the largest category and the total. | Independently read 6, 5, 2 and 4, identify bus riders as the largest group and add the values to get 17 pupils. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask whether the top row always has the highest count. | Explain that the longest bar indicates the highest count, whichever row it occupies. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils read all four values, named the largest category and found the total. Revisit the end of each bar with pupils who read the row position as a count.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U08-04 · Solve Everyday Problems With Data

**Standards** · CS 8.3 · LS 8.3.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 97–99 · Activity Book pp. 153–154
**Learning objective** · Use a table or bar chart to answer a total, a difference and a decision question, giving a reason for the decision.
**Materials** · Teacher-made charts with one-unit scales, a tally table, question cards and individual response slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a chart about lunchtime games and ask which bars could help choose a game for the class. | Identify the relevant bars and suggest comparing their counts. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model finding relevant values in a tally table and a bar chart. Contrast a question asking how many altogether with one asking how many more. | Identify the needed categories, read their values and explain when to add or subtract. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a chart with eight votes for chess, six for draughts and three for puzzles. Ask for the total, the difference between chess and puzzles, and which game to buy if the rule is to buy the most requested. | Independently give 17 votes, a difference of 5 votes and choose chess because its 8 votes exceed both other counts. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why the total vote count cannot identify the most requested game by itself. | Explain that the decision needs a comparison of the individual category counts. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils answered all three questions and justified the decision using the data. Revisit marking only the relevant bars with pupils who used the wrong categories.
