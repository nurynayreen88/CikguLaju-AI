## DLP-MAT-Y2-U02-18 · Create Addition and Subtraction Stories

**Standards** · CS 2.5 · LS 2.5.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 76–77 · Activity Book pp. 52–53  
**Learning objective** · Create two realistic stories, one for addition and one for subtraction, using both numbers in the given roles and ending with a suitable question.  
**Materials** · Number-sentence cards, classroom item pictures and story planning cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show two baskets of the same item. Ask for a question that requires finding the combined total. | Make a question asking how many items there are altogether. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model a join story and a take-away story. Highlight which quantities are given and which the question asks for. | Draft one story with a partner, then swap stories and check that each matches the number sentence. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 236 + 147 and 560 − 128. Check that both numbers have correct roles, the unit is consistent and the question asks for the intended unknown. | Independently write or dictate two stories and include a matching question for each. |
| Closure · 5 min | Read a story that uses a subtraction question with an addition card. Ask for a correction. | Revise the question or the action so the story matches the card. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils created two stories that met all checks. Practise stating the starting amount and change with pupils who reversed them.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U02-19 · Solve Addition and Subtraction Stories

**Standards** · CS 2.5 · LS 2.5.2  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 78–80 · Activity Book pp. 54–55  
**Learning objective** · Choose the correct operation and solve two everyday problems using a representation and a sentence with the correct unit.  
**Materials** · Bar-model strips, base-ten blocks and problem cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Describe two groups bringing craft paper. Ask which quantities are the parts and what is unknown. | Identify the two parts and the missing whole. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model a part-part-whole diagram and a comparison diagram. Ask pupils to choose the operation from what is unknown. | Draw a bar for each practice problem, write the number sentence and check if the result is reasonable. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask for the total when classes bring 245 and 178 cards. Ask how many more cards there are in a box of 507 than in a box of 368. Check both equations, representations and answer sentences. | Independently solve both problems and label each answer in cards. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how to check a difference of 139 between 507 and 368. | Add 139 to 368 to check the larger total. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils solved both problems with valid representations and units. Use aligned bars to reteach comparison where needed.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U02-20 · Create Multiplication and Division Stories

**Standards** · CS 2.5 · LS 2.5.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 116–117 · Activity Book pp. 83–84  
**Learning objective** · Create one equal-groups multiplication story and one grouping division story that correctly interprets a remainder.  
**Materials** · Blank story cards, counters and cups.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show four trays with six counters each. Ask for a question that uses the groups. | Ask how many counters there are altogether. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model writing a question for equal groups. Then model a grouping story and show how the leftover amount fits the context. | Match each story to an arrangement of counters. Swap a draft with a partner to check that the groups and leftover are realistic. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 5 × 4 = 20 and 29 ÷ 6 = 4 remainder 5. Check that both stories assign quantities correctly and ask about the intended unknown. | Independently write or dictate one story for each equation. Include the question about full groups and leftover items in the division story. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why the five leftover items cannot fill another group of six. | Explain the remainder in terms of the story. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils created two consistent stories and interpreted the remainder correctly. Give a concrete grouping prompt where the story roles were unclear.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U02-21 · Solve Multiplication and Division Stories

**Standards** · CS 2.5 · LS 2.5.2  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 118–121 · Activity Book pp. 85–86 (section reference printed on p. 120)  
**Learning objective** · Select the operation and solve two equal-group problems, interpreting the quotient and remainder in the division problem.  
**Materials** · Situation cards, counters, square grids and number-sentence slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show several packs holding the same number of items and a separate pile to be packed. Ask what is already known in each situation. | Identify group size and group count in the first case, and the total and pack size in the second. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model an array for an equal-group total and a grouping diagram for packing a fixed total. Use a related fact to check each calculation. | Choose a model, solve one of each type with a partner and explain what the answer counts. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask for the number of seedlings in 7 trays of 4. Then ask how many full packs of 6 pencils can be made from 38 and how many pencils remain. Check the equations, units and interpretation. | Independently draw a model for each, calculate both answers and state the full packs and leftovers in words. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what an answer of 6 remainder 2 means in the pencil problem. | State that it gives six full packs and two loose pencils. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils chose both operations and interpreted both answers correctly. Use concrete groups for pupils who confused full packs with the number per pack.
