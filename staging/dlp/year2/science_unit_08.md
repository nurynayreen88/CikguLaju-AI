# Science (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 8 Mixture

Seven 60-minute RPH for CS 8.1. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-8-source-check).

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U08-01 · Describe Ways to Separate Mixtures

**Standards** · CS 8.1 · LS 8.1.1
**References** · Textbook pp. 87–94 · Activity Book p. 55
**Learning objective** · Name and describe a suitable method for at least four of five pictured mixtures.
**Materials** · Trays, large coloured counters, dry sand and pebbles, sieve, large steel washers, wand magnet, cork, glass marbles, chalk in water and filter paper.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show two colours of counters in one tray and ask how they could be sorted. | Suggest a method and demonstrate removing one colour. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Set up examples of hand-picking, sieving, magnetic attraction, flotation and filtration. Demonstrate what each method removes or retains. | Visit the stations, match methods to mixtures and describe where each separated material ends up. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give picture cards showing mixed buttons, sand and pebbles, steel and plastic pieces, cork and stones in water, and fine chalk particles in water. Ask for a method and an action for each. | Independently name the five methods and describe at least four correctly, such as passing fine sand through the sieve while retaining the pebbles. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what pupils can see after the sand passes through the sieve. | Identify the sand below the sieve and the pebbles remaining above it. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils described at least four suitable methods. Repeat a demonstration where pupils confused what passed through the tool with what stayed behind.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U08-02 · Explain Why a Separation Method Works

**Standards** · CS 8.1 · LS 8.1.2
**References** · Textbook pp. 89–94 · Activity Book pp. 56–57
**Learning objective** · Choose suitable methods for three mixtures and explain each choice using a difference between the materials.
**Materials** · Sand and pebbles, large steel washers and plastic counters, cork and glass marbles, a sieve, wand magnet, water tray and picture slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Hold up a sieve and a magnet beside a mixture of sand and pebbles. Ask which tool is useful here and why. | Choose the sieve and point to the difference in particle size. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Compare size, magnetic attraction and floating behaviour using the three mixtures. Model explaining a result by linking the property to what the tool does. | Select a tool or method, test it and discuss which difference helped separate the materials. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give fresh pictures of grit mixed with large beads, steel clips mixed with plastic pieces, and cork mixed with stones in water. Ask pupils to explain each choice. | Independently select sieving, magnetic attraction and flotation. Explain the size difference, attraction of steel and contrast between floating and sinking. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why a magnet did not help with cork and stones. | Explain that the chosen materials do not respond to the classroom magnet and recall the property that did help. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils linked all three choices to the relevant properties. Revisit the station with pupils who named a tool without explaining the material difference.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U08-03 · Investigate Which Materials Dissolve in Water

**Standards** · CS 8.1 · LS 8.1.3
**References** · Textbook pp. 94–96 · Activity Book p. 58
**Learning objective** · Record observations for four materials and classify all four as dissolving or remaining visible under the test conditions.
**Materials** · Four labelled clear cups, equal water portions, small measured amounts of sugar, salt, sand and rice, separate stirrers and a results table.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show salt before and after it dissolves in a glass. Ask whether the salt is still present. | Describe what can be seen and suggest that the salt is mixed into the water. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use equal volumes, small portions of each material, equal stirring and the same observation time. Check that pupils record particles that settle as still visible. | Test each sample, note whether particles remain, and compare results with a partner. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask each pupil for an observation and classification in each row. Check that sugar and salt have dissolved and that sand and rice remain visible in the prepared test. | Independently record four observations, then use them to group sugar and salt together and sand and rice together. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what happened to the salt when it could no longer be seen. | Explain that it dissolved and remained mixed in the water. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils used all four observations to classify the samples. Repeat the check for settled particles with pupils who treated them as dissolved.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U08-04 · Investigate the Effect of Water Temperature

**Standards** · CS 8.1 · LS 8.1.4
**References** · Textbook pp. 96–97 · Activity Book p. 59 (temperature comparison)
**Learning objective** · Compare dissolving times for equal sugar samples in cool and warm water and draw a conclusion supported by both results.
**Materials** · Clear cups, cool water and teacher-prepared comfortably warm water, equal masses of the same sugar, identical stirrers, timer and record slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show the two cups and ask which condition might make the same sugar dissolve sooner. | Predict the result and identify the temperature difference. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Keep water volume, sugar mass, grain size and stirring the same. Start both trials together and model timing until no grains remain visible. | Observe both cups, record each time and compare the readings. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask pupils to write both times, circle the shorter one and make a conclusion about temperature. Check the conclusion against the trial records. | Independently report both readings and say which condition dissolved the sugar sooner in the trial. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why using more sugar in one cup would affect the comparison. | Explain that both cups need the same amount so that temperature is the difference being tested. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils drew a conclusion supported by both readings. Repeat the timing demonstration with pupils who confused a shorter time with slower dissolving.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U08-05 · Investigate the Effect of Stirring

**Standards** · CS 8.1 · LS 8.1.4
**References** · Textbook pp. 97–98 · Activity Book p. 61
**Learning objective** · Compare stirred and unstirred samples and support a conclusion with observations from both cups at the same time.
**Materials** · Two clear cups, equal portions of water and table salt, stirrer, timer and a two-column observation slip.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a spoon beside a cup with salt at the bottom. Ask how to check whether stirring makes a difference. | Suggest comparing one stirred cup with a second cup left still. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Keep water temperature, volume and salt mass the same. Ask groups to stir one cup steadily and observe both cups at agreed intervals. | Record the visible grains in each cup at the same times and compare what remains. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask pupils to record both observations at a time when the cups differ and explain which sample dissolved faster. Repeat the demonstration if the first trial gives no clear comparison. | Independently cite what remained visible in each cup and draw a conclusion about stirring. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why the group observed both cups at the same times. | Explain that a comparison needs observations from the same stage of the test. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils used observations from both cups to explain the effect of stirring. Review how to compare evidence from the same time point.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U08-06 · Investigate the Effect of Particle Size

**Standards** · CS 8.1 · LS 8.1.4
**References** · Textbook pp. 98–99 · Activity Book p. 60
**Learning objective** · Compare the time for equal masses of fine and coarse sugar to dissolve and explain the result using the recorded times.
**Materials** · Equal masses of sugar prepared from two identical cubes, one crushed finer than the other, clear cups, water, stirrers, timer and a record sheet.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Display the two sugar portions and ask what is different and what has stayed the same. | Identify the different particle sizes and confirm that the portions have the same mass. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Keep water volume and temperature fixed. Have both pupils stir at the same pace and start timing together. Check that they use the same endpoint. | Time each sample until the grains are no longer visible and record both results. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask each pupil to report the two times, choose the faster sample and identify one factor kept the same. | Independently make the comparison and explain that the finer sugar dissolved sooner when the measurements support it. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what the test would show if the finer sample also used much warmer water. | Explain that the effects of temperature and particle size would be mixed. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils compared both times and identified a controlled factor. Repeat the equal-mass setup with pupils who used different amounts.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U08-07 · Explain Observations About Mixtures

**Standards** · CS 8.1 · LS 8.1.5
**References** · Textbook pp. 96–100 · Activity Book p. 62
**Learning objective** · Communicate two observations about mixtures and give an evidence-based explanation for each using a sketch, caption or oral response.
**Materials** · Class records from a sieving trial and the stirring investigation, a sample sketch and individual response slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a sieve with pebbles and a tray of sand underneath. Ask for one observation and then a reason. | Describe where each material ended up and suggest why. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Review the sieve and a paired stirred–unstirred record. Model labelling the evidence before explaining each result. | Discuss which evidence supports each explanation and practise showing it in a labelled sketch or sentence. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask pupils to explain the separation of sand and pebbles and one recorded effect of stirring. Check that each explanation refers to what was observed. | Independently communicate both observations and explain them using the sieve openings or the two dissolving results. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask pupils to point to one part of a response that gives evidence. | Identify a detail that supports their explanation. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils explained both observations using evidence. Revisit the class records with pupils who gave a conclusion without a matching observation.
