# Mathematics (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 4 Money

Eleven 60-minute RPH for CS 4.1 to 4.7. Textbook references use Part 2. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-4-source-check).

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U04-01 · Recognise Malaysian Banknotes

**Standards** · CS 4.1 · LS 4.1.1  
**References** · Textbook p. 20 · Activity Book p. 103  
**Learning objective** · Identify at least five of six Malaysian banknote denominations up to RM100 and give the printed number as evidence for one.  
**Materials** · Denomination cards and classroom images of RM1, RM5, RM10, RM20, RM50 and RM100 banknotes.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Display RM10 and RM50 images. Ask where pupils can find the value on each. | Point to the printed number and read the value. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model reading the number and the RM symbol. Distribute shuffled images and denomination labels to pairs. | Match each label to its banknote, then arrange the six labels from least to greatest value. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Provide an individual sheet with the six banknotes in a new order. Ask pupils to label the values and circle one number they used as a clue. | Independently write the denominations and mark the clue on one banknote image. |
| Closure · 5 min | Hold up the RM20 and RM100 images for a quick recognition check. | Read both values aloud. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils identified at least five denominations and marked a valid clue. Revisit any confused pair with side-by-side images.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U04-02 · Find and Make Money Values

**Standards** · CS 4.1 · LS 4.1.2  
**References** · Textbook pp. 21–22 · Activity Book p. 104  
**Learning objective** · Find the total value of one set of notes and coins and make a given value in two different ways, within RM100.  
**Materials** · Play-money denominations, value cards and two-column recording sheets headed Ringgit and Sen.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show one RM10 card and two RM5 cards. Ask which group has greater value. | Count the value in each group and explain that both are worth RM10. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model sorting play money by denomination before counting. Set a target of RM26.50 and give pairs different sets of notes and coins to build it. | Count ringgit and sen, build RM26.50 and compare two valid combinations. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show RM20, RM5, RM1, 50 sen and 20 sen. Then ask pupils to make RM16.50 in two ways. Check the total and both combinations. | Independently record RM26.70 for the set shown and list two different combinations totalling RM16.50. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how two sets of money with different numbers of pieces can have the same value. | Explain that the values of the notes and coins must add to the same amount. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils found the correct total and made both combinations. Revisit grouping ringgit and sen where needed.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U04-03 · Add Two Money Amounts

**Standards** · CS 4.2 · LS 4.2.1  
**References** · Textbook pp. 23–25 · Activity Book pp. 105–106 (shared addition reference)  
**Learning objective** · Add two money amounts correctly in two questions, including one that requires exchanging 100 sen for RM1.  
**Materials** · Fictional price cards, play money and a ringgit-and-sen place-value chart.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show price tags of RM4 and RM3. Ask for the cost of buying both items. | Find the total and describe which operation they used. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model adding RM12.45 and RM8.70. Demonstrate the exchange when the sen total is 115 and record RM21.15. | Build the amounts, exchange 100 sen for RM1 and record the aligned calculation. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give RM18.75 + RM23.50 and RM46 + 80 sen. Check alignment, the exchange where needed and both totals. | Independently show the work and write RM42.25 and RM46.80. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask when an exchange from sen to ringgit is needed. | Explain that 100 sen can be exchanged for RM1. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils calculated both totals and handled the exchange. Repeat the exchange with play money for pupils who need it.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U04-04 · Add Three Money Amounts

**Standards** · CS 4.2 · LS 4.2.2  
**References** · Textbook pp. 24–25 · Activity Book pp. 105–106 (shared addition reference)  
**Learning objective** · Add three money amounts in two questions within RM100 and show the grouping used in one solution.  
**Materials** · Three-item order slips and a place-value chart.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Display three fictional prices and ask how to make sure every price is counted. | Point to all three amounts and propose a way to group them. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model adding RM14.60, RM9.40 and RM20.75. Use the first two prices to make RM24 before adding the third. | Circle the first pair, find its subtotal and combine it with the third amount. Compare with a vertical calculation. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give RM18.25 + RM6.75 + RM31.40 and RM20.50 + RM14.85 + RM8.25. Check both totals and one recorded grouping. | Independently write RM56.40 and RM43.60, showing the grouped pair and subtotal in at least one question. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask pupils to show where the third amount appears in the final step. | Point to the amount and explain how they included it. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils found both totals and showed a valid grouping. Use three tick boxes to help pupils account for every amount.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U04-05 · Subtract Two Money Amounts

**Standards** · CS 4.3 · LS 4.3.1  
**References** · Textbook pp. 26–28 · Activity Book pp. 107–108 (shared subtraction reference)  
**Learning objective** · Find change and a price difference correctly, showing a ringgit-to-sen exchange when needed.  
**Materials** · Fictional receipts, price cards and play-money tokens.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show an RM10 payment for a RM6 purchase. Ask what amount the buyer should get back. | Count up from RM6 to RM10 and name the change. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model finding change from RM50 for a RM37.65 purchase. Exchange RM1 for 100 sen, then check the change by addition. | Work in pairs to record RM12.35 change and confirm it by adding it to the price. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask for change from RM40 after paying RM26.80 and for the difference between RM29.50 and RM18.75. Check the equations and the exchange. | Independently write RM13.20 change and RM10.75 difference with aligned working. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask pupils to check one change answer using the receipt amount. | Add the purchase price and change to check the starting amount. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils found both answers and showed the exchange where needed. Revisit how to record the exchanged RM1.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U04-06 · Subtract Two Amounts in Sequence

**Standards** · CS 4.3 · LS 4.3.2  
**References** · Textbook pp. 27–28 · Activity Book pp. 107–108 (shared subtraction reference)  
**Learning objective** · Subtract two payments consecutively from a starting amount up to RM100 and record both intermediate and final balances in two scenarios.  
**Materials** · Two-receipt cards and balance strips with spaces for starting amount, first balance and final balance.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show an RM30 balance with two receipts. Ask where the second subtraction should start. | Point to the balance after the first purchase. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model starting with RM80 and paying RM26.40 then RM18.75. Record RM53.60 after the first payment and RM34.85 after the second. | Use a balance strip to explain each subtraction to a partner. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give RM75 with payments of RM12.80 and RM24.50, and RM60 with payments of RM9.35 and RM16.40. Check both balances in each story. | Independently record RM62.20 then RM37.70, and RM50.65 then RM34.25. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which number changed between the first and second subtractions. | Explain why the first balance becomes the starting amount for the second step. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils recorded all four balances correctly. Reuse the labelled strip with pupils who restarted from the original amount.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U04-07 · Multiply Money Amounts

**Standards** · CS 4.4 · LS 4.4.1  
**References** · Textbook pp. 29–30 · Activity Book p. 109  
**Learning objective** · Calculate two money totals up to RM100 using multiplication, one with a single-digit multiplier and one with 10.  
**Materials** · Identical price cards, item grouping mats and play-money tokens.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show three identical items at RM2 each. Ask how to represent the total with equal groups. | Make three groups of RM2 and state the total. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model five groups of RM6.40. Link repeated addition to RM6.40 × 5 and regroup 200 sen as RM2. | Build the five groups and record the total of RM32.00. Compare their model with the multiplication equation. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give four items at RM7.35 each and ten at RM8.25 each. Check the equation and total in each question. | Independently write RM7.35 × 4 = RM29.40 and RM8.25 × 10 = RM82.50. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what the multiplier represents in either question. | Identify the number of equal-priced items. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils wrote two valid equations and totals. Revisit the matching of item count and price with a grouping mat.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U04-08 · Divide Money Into Equal Shares

**Standards** · CS 4.5 · LS 4.5.1  
**References** · Textbook pp. 31–32 · Activity Book p. 110  
**Learning objective** · Divide two amounts within RM100 into equal shares, using a single-digit divisor and 10, and check one result by multiplication.  
**Materials** · Play-money tokens, sharing mats and equal-share situation cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show RM12 to be shared equally among three groups. Ask what makes the sharing fair. | Give each group the same amount and state each share. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model sharing RM27.60 among three groups. Exchange the ringgit and sen as needed to give each group RM9.20. | Draw the groups, record RM27.60 ÷ 3 = RM9.20 and check by combining the shares. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask pupils to share RM34.20 among six groups and RM72.50 among ten groups. Check both shares and one multiplication check. | Independently calculate RM5.70 and RM7.25 per group, then multiply one share by its group count. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what the quotient tells us in an equal-sharing question. | State that it gives the amount for each group. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils found both shares and checked one by multiplication. Revisit exchanging money before sharing the remaining value.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U04-09 · Plan Spending and Savings

**Standards** · CS 4.6 · LS 4.6.1  
**References** · Textbook p. 33 · Activity Book p. 111  
**Learning objective** · Explain the purpose of spending and saving and prepare a fictional RM60 plan that includes at least RM20 in savings and accounts for all RM60.  
**Materials** · Fictional budget cards, spending choices and a two-column spending-and-savings worksheet.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a fictional child with RM50, a school supply to buy and a future savings goal. Ask where the money could go. | Suggest one current expense and one reason to save. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model reserving RM20 for savings, spending RM18 on supplies and placing the unused RM12 with the savings. Show that RM18 and RM32 account for RM50. | Compare two spending options and discuss how each affects the amount available to save. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a fictional RM60 budget, supplies costing RM24 and an optional RM14 item. Ask each pupil for a plan with a reason. Check inclusion of supplies, total and the savings goal. | Independently record spending of RM24 or RM38, savings of RM36 or RM22 respectively, and one reason for saving. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what happens to the savings amount when the optional item is bought. | Compare the two plans and state the difference in savings. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils accounted for RM60, met the savings goal and gave a reason. Revisit allocating any unspent amount in the budget.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U04-10 · Solve Shopping and Change Problems

**Standards** · CS 4.7 · LS 4.7.1  
**References** · Textbook pp. 34–36 · Activity Book p. 112 (shared problem-solving reference)  
**Learning objective** · Select the appropriate operations and solve two everyday money problems up to RM100, including one that requires finding a total before change.  
**Materials** · Fictional stationery receipts and a worksheet with space for diagrams, number sentences and answers.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a price tag and an amount paid. Ask which number tells us the cost and which tells us how much money was handed over. | Identify the two quantities and describe how they are related. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model paying RM50 for items priced RM19.60 and RM13.75. Ask what must be found first and show the RM16.65 change using two equations. | Mark the facts, find the total and explain why the next step uses subtraction. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give two stories: a purchase of RM24.80 and RM9.65 paid with RM50, and two bags priced RM38.50 and RM26.90 asking for the difference. | Independently write suitable equations and answer RM15.55 change and RM11.60 difference in context. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how to check the change answer against the amount paid. | Add the cost and change and compare the result with RM50. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils chose the appropriate operations and solved both stories. Ask pupils who missed a step to underline the question before recalculating.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U04-11 · Solve Equal-Group Money Problems

**Standards** · CS 4.7 · LS 4.7.1  
**References** · Textbook pp. 34–37 · Activity Book p. 112 (shared problem-solving reference)  
**Learning objective** · Choose multiplication or division for two everyday money problems and label the total or equal share correctly.  
**Materials** · Fictional shop cards, group diagrams and equal-share story cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Display four equal-priced items and a separate picture of one amount shared by four groups. Ask what is known in each situation. | Identify the price of one item in the first situation and the total in the second. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Compare three items at RM7.50 each with RM22.50 shared equally by three groups. Draw equal groups and connect the two calculations. | Match multiplication to the total and division to each share. Explain their matches. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask for the total for six items at RM8.45 each and each share when RM68.40 is divided among four groups. Check operations, calculations and labels. | Independently find RM50.70 in total and RM17.10 per group. Explain the operation chosen in one problem. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what fact tells us whether to find a total or a share. | Point to the known quantity and the question in one story. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils chose the correct operations and labelled both answers. Revisit a pair of inverse stories using the same group diagram.
