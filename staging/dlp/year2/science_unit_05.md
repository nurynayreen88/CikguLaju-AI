# Science (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 5 Plants

Six 60-minute RPH for CS 5.1. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-5-source-check).

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U05-01 · Explain Why Plants Are Important

**Standards** · CS 5.1 · LS 5.1.1
**References** · Textbook pp. 49–50 · Activity Book p. 29
**Learning objective** · Give one example of how a plant helps people and one of how a plant helps an animal, with the benefit named in each example.
**Materials** · Pictures of rice, a mango tree, a bird nest in a tree and a caterpillar feeding on leaves; two-column recording slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a rice plant beside a bowl of rice and ask how the pictures are connected. | Describe the link between the plant and the food. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model a benefit statement using the rice pictures. Give pairs pictures of the tree, nest and feeding caterpillar and ask what each animal gets from a plant. | Sort the examples into food and shelter, then explain a picture using “The plant provides … for …”. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show a mango fruit eaten by a person and a bird sheltering in a tree. Check one correct benefit for each recipient. | Independently write or say that the mango tree provides food for a person and that the tree provides shelter for the bird. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what the caterpillar gets from the plant and how pupils can tell. | Name food as the benefit and point to the feeding evidence. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils named both recipients and their benefits correctly. Revisit the picture clues with pupils who named a plant without a benefit.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U05-02 · Identify What Seeds Need to Germinate

**Standards** · CS 5.1 · LS 5.1.2
**References** · Textbook p. 51 · Activity Book pp. 30–31
**Learning objective** · State the three conditions needed for seed germination and identify the missing condition in two illustrated examples.
**Materials** · Dry and germinated mung beans; teacher-made condition cards showing moist cotton at room temperature, dry cotton, reduced access to air and a cold location.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Compare a dry seed with a germinated seed. Ask what visible change tells us germination has started. | Point to the root emerging from the seed and describe the change. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Introduce water, air and a suitable temperature. Show a reference condition and compare one changed condition at a time using the cards. | Match condition cards to water, air or temperature. Predict which condition is suitable and explain why. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give each pupil a dry-cotton case and a cold-location case with the other conditions stated. Ask pupils to identify each missing condition and list all three needs. | Independently identify water and a suitable temperature in the respective cases, then state all three germination needs. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what “suitable temperature” means for the seed shown. | Explain that the seed needs a temperature at which it can germinate. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils identified both missing conditions and stated all three needs. Review the condition cards with pupils who confuse a seed's needs with those of a growing plant.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U05-03 · Record Changes From Seed to Seedling

**Standards** · CS 5.1 · LS 5.1.3
**References** · Textbook pp. 52–53 · Activity Book p. 32
**Learning objective** · Record the height and number of visible leaves at three observed stages of mung-bean growth and describe two changes using the records.
**Materials** · Living mung-bean specimens prepared in advance at three stages, labels showing days since sowing, rulers and individual observation tables.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a newly germinated seed and an older seedling. Ask what could be measured or counted. | Suggest comparing the height or counting visible leaves. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Demonstrate measuring each specimen from its support surface to the shoot tip and agree which visible leaves to count. Guide groups to visit the labelled specimens in order. | Observe the real seeds and seedlings, measure and count using the same method, then compare observations within the group. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask each pupil to complete a three-row table and describe two changes supported by their own observations. Check the units and the match between each conclusion and its data. | Record the day, height in centimetres and leaf count, then compare two stages using the recorded values. |
| Closure · 5 min | Invite a pupil to point to the values that support one observed change. | Read out the values and describe the change. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils completed the three records and described two supported changes. Repeat the measurement or counting method where results were inconsistent.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U05-04 · Sequence the Stages of Plant Growth

**Standards** · CS 5.1 · LS 5.1.4
**References** · Textbook pp. 54–55 · Activity Book pp. 33–34
**Learning objective** · Arrange five stages of a flowering plant's growth and explain one visible change between adjacent stages.
**Materials** · Labelled tomato sequence for modelling, shuffled okra stage cards and a five-box recording strip.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a flower and a fruit from the same kind of plant. Ask which appeared first. | Predict the order and give a reason. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model using a visible clue to place a germinating seed after a seed. Give groups the tomato cards and ask them to check each pair of neighbouring stages. | Arrange seed, germination, young plant, flowering and fruiting. Point to a clue that distinguishes each neighbouring pair. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give each pupil shuffled okra pictures. Check the five positions and one explanation that uses picture evidence. | Independently order and label the pictures, then explain a change such as flowers appearing after the young plant stage. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which picture shows that the seed has started to grow. | Identify the germination picture and point to the emerging root. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils ordered all five stages and explained a change. Revisit any swapped stages using the visual clue on each card.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U05-05 · Investigate What Plants Need to Grow

**Standards** · CS 5.1 · LS 5.1.5
**References** · Textbook pp. 56–58 · Activity Book p. 35
**Learning objective** · Compare two plants in a fair watering investigation and use a recorded observation to explain why water matters, then name air and sunlight as further needs and nutrients as supporting growth.
**Materials** · Two similar seedlings in identical pots, dated class observations from a seven-day investigation, labels, a measuring cup and a comparison sheet.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show the two plants and ask which differences pupils can observe. | Describe the leaves or stem of each plant. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Review the class investigation. Both pots received the same light and growing medium, with watering as the changed condition. Ask pupils to compare the first- and seventh-day records. Use the textbook results to discuss air, sunlight and the role of nutrients. | Compare the live plants and the records, identify what changed and what was kept the same, then point to evidence for the effect of watering. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask each pupil to complete one evidence sentence about water and label a plant diagram with its further needs and the role of nutrients. | Independently describe a recorded difference, link it to watering, name air and sunlight, and explain that nutrients help plants grow. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why both pots were kept in the same place. | Explain that the comparison used the same light for both plants. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils drew the water conclusion from evidence and identified the other requirements. Compare the original records again where pupils give a prediction as an observation.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U05-06 · Explain Observations About a Plant's Life

**Standards** · CS 5.1 · LS 5.1.6
**References** · Textbook pp. 57–59 · Activity Book p. 36
**Learning objective** · Explain two observations from a plant record and use them to predict one effect of interrupted growth on a person or an animal.
**Materials** · Dated class sketches or photos of a seed and seedling, pictures of a healthy and a wilted plant, and a three-box storyboard.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show two dated plant observations and ask which changes can be seen. | Identify a visible change between the records. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model how to connect an observation to an explanation. Compare conditions for germination with those for later growth and discuss an example of a plant used for food. | In pairs, rehearse a short explanation using two dated records and predict what could happen if plants stop growing before they produce food. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask pupils to explain two observations and one consequence using a labelled sketch, short writing or an oral explanation. Check the distinction between observations and prediction. | Independently explain two visible changes using the records and predict a consequence, such as fewer leaves available for a leaf-eating animal. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which statement came directly from the observation and which described a possible future outcome. | Identify one observation and the prediction based on it. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils explained two supported observations and gave a plausible prediction. Use the storyboard to revisit any missing link.
