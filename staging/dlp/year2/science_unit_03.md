# Science (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 3 Humans

Five 60-minute RPH for CS 3.1. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-3-source-check).

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U03-01 · Human Reproduction

**Standards** · CS 3.1 · LS 3.1.1  
**References** · Textbook p. 24 · Activity Book p. 13  
**Learning objective** · State that humans reproduce by giving birth and select the picture that shows a new human entering a family.  
**Materials** · Teacher-made cards showing a newborn with a caregiver, an older child, and an unrelated picture of an egg hatching.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a picture of a newborn with a caregiver. Ask which person has just been born. | Point to the newborn and describe what they see. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Introduce the words human, newborn and giving birth. Compare the two human cards and ask which represents the arrival of a new baby. | Sort the pictures, match the newborn card to the phrase “giving birth” and say how humans reproduce. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show three new drawings: a newborn with a caregiver, a child reading and a chick hatching. Ask “Which drawing shows a human who has just been born?” and “How do humans reproduce?” | Independently select the newborn picture and answer the second question in a sentence. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask for the key sentence again and connect it to the next lesson on changes after birth. | State that humans reproduce by giving birth. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils selected the correct picture and stated how humans reproduce. Revisit the vocabulary using the newborn card.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U03-02 · Describe Growth Since Birth

**Standards** · CS 3.1 · LS 3.1.2  
**References** · Textbook pp. 25–27 · Activity Book pp. 14–15  
**Learning objective** · Use the records for one fictional child to describe the changes in body size, height and weight between birth and age seven.  
**Materials** · Stage cards and a fictional record showing 50 cm and 3 kg at birth and 120 cm and 23 kg at age seven, with two illustrations of the same child.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show the child's two pictures. Ask how the earlier and later stages differ. | Identify the baby and the older child and name one visible change. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model reading a two-row growth record. Ask which column shows height and which shows weight. Use pictures to discuss body size. | Put the stage cards in order and use the record and pictures to make one statement about each change. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a new fictional example, with 49 cm and 3 kg at birth and 117 cm and 21 kg at age seven. Check all three statements against the pictures and record. | Independently describe the changes in body size, height and weight. Cite one pair of readings as evidence. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how to distinguish what was seen in the picture from what was read in the record. | Identify the evidence used for body size and for one measured change. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils described all three changes and cited one pair of readings. Revisit the units with pupils who confused height and weight.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U03-03 · Compare Growth Among Individuals

**Standards** · CS 3.1 · LS 3.1.3  
**References** · Textbook pp. 28–30 · Activity Book p. 16  
**Learning objective** · Compare two sets of measurements for fictional children of the same age and use at least two findings to conclude that individuals can grow differently.  
**Materials** · Teacher-made paper hand outlines with pre-checked lengths, rulers and anonymised model records. Do not collect pupils' own body measurements.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show two different hand outlines labelled “Age 7”. Ask whether equal age means equal size. | Suggest a measurement that could test the idea. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model measuring hand length from a marked wrist line to the longest fingertip. Give pairs two outlines and the matching fictional height records. | Measure both outlines, record the lengths and compare them alongside the heights. Discuss why one measurement cannot describe all growth. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a fresh pair of outlines measuring 12 cm and 14 cm, with heights of 123 cm and 118 cm. Check two accurate comparisons and an evidence-based conclusion. | Independently measure the outlines and write one comparison about hand length, one about height and one conclusion. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what the examples show about children of the same age. | Explain that their measurements can differ and point to one result. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils used two accurate findings to support the conclusion. Repeat one comparison using a side-by-side table where needed.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U03-04 · Recognise Inherited Features

**Standards** · CS 3.1 · LS 3.1.4, 3.1.5  
**References** · Textbook pp. 30–32 · Activity Book pp. 17–20 (shared section reference)  
**Learning objective** · Explain that features can be inherited from parents or earlier generations and identify two possible inherited features in a fictional family chart.  
**Materials** · Illustrated fictional family charts and feature cards for iris colour, hair type and skin colour.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show two fictional relatives with the same hair type. Ask pupils to describe the feature. | Describe the shared hair type using the illustration. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model reading a fictional family chart across two generations. Explain that a shared feature may be inherited and that a picture alone cannot tell which relative it came from. | Circle two shared features and discuss why clothing or a hairstyle chosen for the day is a different kind of similarity. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a fictional chart showing that Aina and her mother have brown irises, and Aina and her grandfather have wavy hair. Check both examples and the explanation. | Independently name two possible inherited features, identify the people who share them and explain the idea in one sentence. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask for one feature pupils can compare on a chart. | Name a feature and point to an example in the fictional chart. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils identified two examples and explained inheritance. Revisit feature categories with a labelled picture.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U03-05 · Communicate Findings About Growth and Heredity

**Standards** · CS 3.1 · LS 3.1.6  
**References** · Textbook pp. 33–34 · Activity Book pp. 19–20  
**Learning objective** · Communicate one growth observation and one possible inherited feature, giving relevant evidence for both from a fictional case.  
**Materials** · Case cards with two dated height readings and a fictional family illustration, pencils and a two-box response sheet.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a height record and a fictional family chart. Ask which source answers a question about change over time. | Choose the height record and explain why it has two readings. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model a growth statement supported by two readings and an inheritance statement supported by a shared feature. Offer a labelled sketch or short oral report. | Prepare a two-part explanation with a partner and check that the evidence matches each statement. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a new case with heights of 104 cm and 121 cm and a chart showing shared curly hair. Check one supported finding of each type. | Independently present both findings through a labelled sketch, two short written statements or an oral explanation. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask pupils to name the evidence that makes one of their statements clear. | Point to the two readings or the shared feature in the chart. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils gave both findings with supporting evidence. Provide the sentence stem “I know this because …” for the missing explanation.
