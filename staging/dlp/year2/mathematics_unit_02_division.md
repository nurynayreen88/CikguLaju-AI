## DLP-MAT-Y2-U02-13 · Share and Group Equally

**Standards** · CS 2.4 · LS 2.4.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 95–98 · Activity Book pp. 71–74  
**Learning objective** · Model and solve at least three of four exact division situations, identifying whether the result means the number of groups or the amount per group.  
**Materials** · Counters, paper cups, group mats and number lines.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show 12 counters and three cups. Ask how to share them equally. | Deal the counters into cups and count how many each cup has. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Contrast sharing 12 among three cups with making groups of three from 12. Show backward jumps of three for the grouping model. | Build both models, record 12 ÷ 3 = 4 and explain what 4 means in each. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give four tasks: share 18 among 3, group 20 in fives, share 24 among 4 and group 28 in sevens. Check the drawings, equations and what each quotient means. | Independently draw each model, write the equation and label the quotient with its meaning. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how a pupil can tell whether a question gives the number of groups or the group size. | Identify the given quantity in one of the examples. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils modelled and labelled at least three solutions correctly. Repeat both interpretations side by side for pupils who confused them.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U02-14 · Use Multiplication to Divide

**Standards** · CS 2.4 · LS 2.4.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 99–103 · Activity Book pp. 75–79  
**Learning objective** · Solve at least five of six exact division facts and write a related multiplication fact to check one answer.  
**Materials** · Fact-family cards, array grids and mini-whiteboards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a 4-by-6 array and cover the row count. Ask how the total and column count help recover it. | Count or use a known fact to find the missing dimension. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use 4 × 6 = 24 to build two division facts. Model finding a missing factor with 42 ÷ 7. | Build fact families with a partner and use the related multiplication fact to check each quotient. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 21 ÷ 3, 36 ÷ 4, 40 ÷ 5, 42 ÷ 6, 56 ÷ 8 and 63 ÷ 9. Check at least five correct quotients and one multiplication check. | Independently solve all six and write a matching product for one. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what multiplication fact checks 54 ÷ 6 = 9. | Give 6 × 9 = 54 and point to 9 as the factor that was missing. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils met both checks. Use an array to reconnect a fact that pupils recalled incorrectly.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U02-15 · Divide by One and Use Zero as the Dividend

**Standards** · CS 2.4 · LS 2.4.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 104–106 · Activity Book p. 80 (shared section reference)  
**Learning objective** · Find at least four of five quotients involving a divisor of one, a zero dividend or equal positive numbers, and explain one case.  
**Materials** · Counters, cups and fact cards. All examples have a non-zero divisor.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Place eight counters in one cup. Ask how many the one cup receives. | Give eight and connect it to 8 ÷ 1. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Compare sharing a set with one recipient, sharing zero among several recipients and placing one counter into each of as many cups as counters. | Act out each case, write the related fact and explain what stays the same. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 6 ÷ 1, 9 ÷ 1, 0 ÷ 5, 0 ÷ 8 and 7 ÷ 7. Check the quotients and one explanation using counters or a matching multiplication fact. | Independently solve the five facts and explain one. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what each of three cups receives when there are no counters to share. | Give zero and connect the answer to 0 ÷ 3. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils gave at least four correct quotients and one explanation. Use empty cups to revisit the zero-dividend case.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U02-16 · Divide With a Remainder

**Standards** · CS 2.4 · LS 2.4.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 109–113  
**Learning objective** · Find the quotient and remainder in at least three of four one-digit-divisor problems and show why one remainder is smaller than the divisor.  
**Materials** · Counters, group mats and basic-fact cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Ask pupils to group 13 counters in fours. Discuss what happens to the loose counter. | Make three full groups and identify the one left over. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use 29 ÷ 6 to find the largest multiple of six below 29. Show how the remainder is what is left after forming the full groups. | Build several groups with counters, then record quotient and remainder and check both using multiplication and addition. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 17 ÷ 3, 23 ÷ 4, 31 ÷ 6 and 43 ÷ 8. Check three or more correct pairs and one explanation about the size of the remainder. | Independently solve each and show why the remainder in one answer cannot make another full group. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask whether 3 remainder 6 can be a finished answer when dividing by five. | Explain that six can make another full group of five. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils calculated at least three quotient-remainder pairs and explained one check. Rebuild a worked example for pupils who stopped before forming all full groups.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U02-17 · Divide Two-Digit Numbers by Ten

**Standards** · CS 2.4 · LS 2.4.2  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 105–107 and 114–115 · Activity Book pp. 81–82 (remainder-section reference)  
**Learning objective** · Find at least three of four quotients and remainders, including one exact division and one with a non-zero remainder, and explain one using tens and ones.  
**Materials** · Ten rods, unit cubes, bundle mats and mini-whiteboards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show 40 counters as four bundles of ten. Ask how many full packs of ten can be made. | Name the four packs and say that none are left. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Contrast 60 ÷ 10 with 64 ÷ 10. Explain that a full ten gives one pack and the loose ones are the remainder. | Model each case using rods and cubes and record the quotient and remainder. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 30 ÷ 10, 54 ÷ 10, 80 ÷ 10 and 97 ÷ 10. Check both types of result and one place-value explanation. | Independently solve all four and draw the full tens and loose ones for one. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why the remainder is zero for 80 ÷ 10. | Explain that all 80 objects fit into eight full packs of ten. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils met the quotient and remainder checks. Revisit the distinction between packs and objects with loose-cube examples.
