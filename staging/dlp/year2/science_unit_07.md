# Science (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 7 Electricity

Seven 60-minute RPH for CS 7.1. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-7-source-check).

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U07-01 · Identify Electrical Components

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.1
**References** · Textbook pp. 73–75 · Activity Book pp. 45–46
**Learning objective** · Identify a dry cell, bulb and switch in a classroom kit and label all three on a picture of a circuit.
**Materials** · Dry cell in a holder, miniature bulb in a holder, switch, connecting wires and circuit pictures.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a battery-powered lamp and ask which parts might be inside. | Suggest parts and compare them with the classroom kit. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Place the three components, holders and wires on a tray. Model distinguishing the cell from its holder and the bulb from its holder. | Match each component to its name, then locate the same components in a picture with a different layout. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Hand out an unlabelled circuit picture. Check that each label points to the component itself. | Independently label the dry cell, bulb and switch. Point to the equivalent components on the tray. |
| Closure · 5 min | Hold up a switch and a wire. Ask which item is the switch. | Identify the switch and distinguish it from the wire. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils identified and labelled all three components. Revisit the physical components with pupils who labelled the holders.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U07-02 · Explain the Functions of Circuit Components

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.2
**References** · Textbook p. 76 · Activity Book p. 47
**Learning objective** · Explain the functions of the dry cell, bulb, switch and wires in a complete circuit, with at least three correct explanations including the switch.
**Materials** · Working low-voltage classroom circuit, function cards and individual response slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Close and open the switch in a working circuit. Ask which component was moved. | Identify the switch and describe the change at the bulb. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Trace the complete path. Describe how the dry cell supplies electrical energy, the bulb gives light, the wires connect the parts and the switch completes or breaks the path. | Match functions to the kit components and explain to a partner how closing and opening the switch affects the path. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give each pupil a four-component picture. Ask what each part does and check the explanation of the switch. | Independently describe each function and state that opening the switch breaks the circuit. |
| Closure · 5 min | Point to a wire between two components and ask what it does. | Explain that it joins the components in the electrical path. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils explained at least three functions, including the switch. Use the working circuit to revisit any mistaken function.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U07-03 · Build a Complete Electric Circuit

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.3
**References** · Textbook pp. 77–78 · Activity Book p. 48
**Learning objective** · Assemble a working circuit using a dry cell, bulb, switch and connecting wires, and demonstrate both the open and closed switch states.
**Materials** · Teacher-checked dry-cell circuit kits with matched miniature bulbs, insulated leads and switches.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a partly assembled kit and ask what path the bulb needs. | Trace a possible path from one dry-cell terminal to the other. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model assembly with the switch open. Check that the path includes the bulb before pupils close the switch. Use the classroom dry-cell kits only. | Assemble the circuit in pairs. Trace the path through the bulb and switch, then observe the bulb with the switch closed and open. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give each pupil a turn to assemble and demonstrate the circuit. Check each required part and both observed states. | Independently connect the kit, show the bulb lighting with the switch closed and show it going out when the switch opens. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask pupils to point out where the path is broken when the bulb goes out. | Point to the gap at the switch and leave the kit switched off. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils assembled a working circuit and demonstrated both states. Rebuild the path with pupils who omitted a required connection.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U07-04 · Predict Why a Bulb Does Not Light

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.4
**References** · Textbook pp. 79–81 · Activity Book pp. 49–50
**Learning objective** · Predict a likely cause for a bulb failing to light in two circuits and propose a check that tests each prediction.
**Materials** · Known-good dry-cell kit, two teacher-prepared faulty setups with an open switch or loose lead, and a picture of a loose bulb.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a working kit and then open the switch. Ask what could make a similar bulb stay dark. | Suggest a break in the path and name a place to check. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Present one fault at a time. Ask for a prediction and a check before making a change. Open the switch before handling leads or the bulb. | Inspect each setup, propose a cause and observe whether correcting that single fault lights the bulb. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a case with a loose lead and a case with a bulb shown loose in its holder. Ask for a cause and a suitable check in each. | Independently name both likely causes and describe checking the lead connection or reseating the bulb while the switch is open. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why pupils should check one possible fault at a time. | Explain how a single change helps identify which part caused the result. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils predicted both faults and proposed relevant checks. Revisit the difference between an observation and its possible cause.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U07-05 · Record What Happens When Objects Replace a Switch

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.5
**References** · Textbook pp. 81–83 · Activity Book p. 51
**Learning objective** · Test four dry objects in the switch position and record whether the bulb lights for each object.
**Materials** · Teacher-checked dry-cell test circuit with bulb and clip leads, uncoated steel washer, metal paper clip, rubber eraser, plastic lid and results table.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show the bulb lighting with a closed switch. Ask what might happen if another object bridges the switch position. | Predict whether a selected object will let the bulb light. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Verify the kit with a known metal sample. Keep the bulb in the circuit and show how to clip each sample into the switch position. Use the same kit for all four objects. | Test the objects one at a time, observe the bulb and record each result as lights or does not light. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask each pupil to show their four-row table and repeat one trial if their observation differs from the group. Check the object names and observations. | Independently complete the table and explain which observation belongs to the repeated trial. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which part of the setup changed between trials. | Identify the sample as the changed object and name one part kept the same. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils recorded the results of all four trials. Recheck clip contact and the working kit with pupils whose records were incomplete.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U07-06 · Classify Conductors and Insulators

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.6
**References** · Textbook pp. 81–83 · Activity Book pp. 52–53
**Learning objective** · Classify four tested objects from their bulb results and state a rule linking each group to the flow of electric current.
**Materials** · Recorded trials from the previous lesson, evidence cards, dry-cell test kit and a two-column classification sheet.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show two records with opposite bulb results. Ask what difference can be observed. | Identify which sample lit the bulb and which did not. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Build a class rule from the trial records. Demonstrate checking the tester and clip contact if an unexpected result occurs. | Group the samples by result and explain which group allowed current through the working test circuit. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Provide checked results for an aluminium tab, steel washer, rubber strip and plastic spoon. Ask each pupil to classify them and explain both groups. | Independently put the two metals under conductors and the rubber and plastic under insulators. State how current flow explains both results. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how to decide which group an unfamiliar object belongs to. | Explain how to test it in the working dry-cell circuit and use the bulb result. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils classified all four objects and explained both groups. Revisit the trial evidence with pupils who sorted by appearance alone.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U07-07 · Explain Observations From a Buzzer Circuit

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.7
**References** · Textbook pp. 76, 81–83 · Activity Book p. 54
**Learning objective** · Communicate the buzzer's behaviour in two switch states and explain the sound using the condition of the circuit.
**Materials** · Teacher-checked low-voltage buzzer matched to the classroom dry-cell kit, switch, insulated leads and individual sketch slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Demonstrate a buzzer briefly in the same type of circuit that previously used a bulb. Ask what pupils can observe. | Identify sound as the buzzer's observable output. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Let pairs trace the path through the buzzer and switch. Demonstrate both switch states and model recording the sound separately from its explanation. | Observe and record sound or silence in both states, then trace the path that accounts for each result. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask each pupil to present two observation–explanation pairs using a sketch, short caption or oral response. Check both switch states and the role of the buzzer. | Independently explain that the buzzer sounds when the circuit is complete and stops when the switch breaks the path. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which component controls whether the buzzer sounds in this setup. | Identify the switch and describe how it changes the path. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils explained both observations using the condition of the circuit. Repeat the demonstration with pupils who described the sound without explaining the path.
