# Science (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 4 Animals

Seven 60-minute RPH for CS 4.1. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-4-source-check).

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U04-01 · How Animals Reproduce

**Standards** · CS 4.1 · LS 4.1.1  
**References** · Textbook pp. 36–37 · Activity Book pp. 21–22 (shared section reference)  
**Learning objective** · State the two ways animals reproduce shown in the textbook and give one correct example of each.  
**Materials** · Picture pairs showing a duck with eggs and a goat with its young, plus picture cards of a crocodile nest and an elephant calf.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a duckling and a kid. Ask how each came into the world. | Make a prediction for each animal. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Reveal the picture evidence and model the phrases “lays eggs” and “gives birth to live young”. Introduce the crocodile and elephant cards and ask which phrase fits each. | Match a phrase to each picture pair and explain their choices to a partner. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Hand out an exit slip showing a duck and an elephant with their young. Ask pupils to state the two ways and label each example. | Independently write or say “lays eggs” and “gives birth to live young”, then match them to the duck and elephant. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask for one example of each way and point to the matching pictures. | State the two examples in full sentences. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils stated both ways and gave a correct example for each. Repeat the picture-to-phrase match where needed.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U04-02 · Classify Animals by Reproduction

**Standards** · CS 4.1 · LS 4.1.2  
**References** · Textbook pp. 36–38 · Activity Book pp. 21–22 (shared section reference)  
**Learning objective** · Classify at least five of six animals by their way of reproducing and justify one choice using a clue.  
**Materials** · Two-column sorting mat and illustrated clues for duck, crocodile, mosquito, goat, bat and elephant.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Hold up a bat and a duck card. Ask what evidence would tell us how each reproduces. | Suggest looking for eggs or evidence of a live birth. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model sorting a duck using a picture of its nest. Give teams the clue cards and ask them to read the evidence before they place each animal. | Sort the cards, then swap one example with another team and explain its placement. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give each pupil a shuffled six-card sheet with short clues. Check for at least five correct placements and one justification. | Independently place the animals in the two groups and explain one choice using its clue. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why a duck and a bat can both fly and still belong in different groups. | Identify the way each reproduces as the sorting rule. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils placed at least five animals correctly and justified one choice. Revisit the evidence on any misplaced card.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U04-03 · Animals That Lay Few or Many Eggs

**Standards** · CS 4.1 · LS 4.1.3  
**References** · Textbook pp. 39–40 · Activity Book p. 23  
**Learning objective** · Use two observation pictures to explain one example of an animal that lays few eggs and one that lays many eggs.  
**Materials** · Swan nest and frogspawn illustrations, counters for the swan nest, and an illustrated observation sheet with a swan and a fish example.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Display a swan nest and a frogspawn picture side by side. Ask which has more visible eggs. | Point to the picture with more eggs and describe the clue. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use counters to represent the eggs in the illustrated swan nest. Show a large cluster of frog eggs and ask pupils to compare the observations. | Make a sketch of each observation and describe which animal laid few eggs and which laid many in the examples. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give pupils a swan-nest illustration and a fish-egg cluster with the animal named. Check one evidence-based sentence for each picture. | Independently name the animals, use “few” or “many” for each and refer to the eggs shown. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask for one pair of examples from the lesson. | Compare the swan and frog examples in one sentence. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils explained both examples with picture evidence. Revisit the few-versus-many comparison with counters.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U04-04 · Animals That Give Birth to Few or Many Young

**Standards** · CS 4.1 · LS 4.1.4  
**References** · Textbook pp. 41–42 · Activity Book p. 24  
**Learning objective** · Give one example each of an animal that gives birth to few and many young, supported by the number shown in an illustrated birth record.  
**Materials** · Model birth records for an elephant with one calf, a rabbit with six kits, a dolphin with one calf and a cat with five kittens.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show an elephant birth record and ask how many young were born in that record. | Count the calf and state the number. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Compare the elephant record with the rabbit record. Ask what the two examples show about the number born at a time. | Fill a two-row table with animal, number born and “few” or “many”. Explain the comparison to a partner. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give the dolphin and cat records. Ask pupils to compare them and check the names and evidence. | Independently write or say that the dolphin is the “few” example and the cat is the “many” example, citing the two counts. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what pupils looked at to decide between “few” and “many”. | State that they compared how many young were born in the examples. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils gave both examples and supported them with the counts shown. Repeat the comparison using counters if needed.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U04-05 · Record Changes During Animal Growth

**Standards** · CS 4.1 · LS 4.1.5  
**References** · Textbook pp. 45–46 · Activity Book pp. 25–27  
**Learning objective** · Sequence four illustrated frog life-cycle stages and record two observable changes between stages.  
**Materials** · Frog and cow life-cycle illustrations, four shuffled frog cards and a recording strip.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a tadpole and an adult frog. Ask pupils to point to one visible difference. | Describe a feature such as the tail or legs. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model how to record the appearance of a stage from an illustration. Use calf and adult cow illustrations to practise recording an observation, then distribute the frog cards. | Sequence frog eggs, tadpole, froglet and adult frog. Discuss the froglet’s legs and the shorter tail, then compare the froglet with the adult frog. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Hand out a fresh four-image frog strip in a different order. Check the sequence and two changes between neighbouring stages. | Independently number the stages and record two changes using arrows, labelled sketches or short phrases. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which pair of images supports one change. | Point to two stages and read out the change. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils ordered four stages and recorded two supported changes. Revisit the pair of stages pupils confused.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U04-06 · Compare Young Animals and Their Adults

**Standards** · CS 4.1 · LS 4.1.6  
**References** · Textbook pp. 43–44 · Activity Book p. 28  
**Learning objective** · Compare two young-and-adult pairs and support each comparison with a visible feature.  
**Materials** · Paired pictures of a kitten and adult cat, a calf and adult cow, a caterpillar and adult butterfly, and a tadpole and adult frog.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show the kitten and adult cat. Ask which features can be seen on both. | Identify a shared feature such as four legs. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model using the same feature to compare both pictures. Contrast the adult butterfly's wings with the caterpillar picture. | Match young animals to their adults and mark a visible similarity or difference on each pair. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give pupils the cow and frog pairs. Check one comparison supported by a feature for each pair. | Independently explain how the calf resembles the cow and how the tadpole differs from the adult frog. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask for one example of a young animal that looks noticeably different from its adult. | Name the example and point to the feature that supports it. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils made two accurate comparisons with visible features. Revisit the comparison using labelled pictures.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U04-07 · Communicate Observations About Reproduction and Growth

**Standards** · CS 4.1 · LS 4.1.7  
**References** · Textbook p. 46 and p. 48 · Teacher-made assessment  
**Learning objective** · Communicate one observation about reproduction and two about growth using evidence from one illustrated life cycle.  
**Materials** · Butterfly life-cycle pictures showing an adult laying eggs, a caterpillar, a chrysalis and an adult butterfly, plus paper for labelled sketches.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a butterfly laying eggs and ask pupils which detail tells us how it reproduces. | Point to the eggs and describe what the adult does. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Demonstrate how to mark the reproduction observation separately from changes in growth on a simple picture sequence. Give pairs a set of cycle pictures. | Rehearse a short report with a partner. Point to the egg-laying image and explain two changes shown across the pictures. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Invite each pupil to make a labelled sketch, write a short report or give an oral account. Check the reproduction observation and two growth observations against the pictures. | Independently communicate that the adult lays eggs, that a caterpillar hatches and that an adult with wings emerges from the chrysalis, or give other supported observations. |
| Closure · 5 min | Invite one pupil to share an observation and show its evidence. | Listen and locate the matching picture. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils communicated all three observations with picture evidence. Offer a three-box prompt for the missing observation.
