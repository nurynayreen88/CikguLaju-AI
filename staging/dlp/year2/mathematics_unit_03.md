# Mathematics (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 3 Fractions and Decimals

Eight 60-minute RPH for CS 3.1–3.4. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-3-source-check).

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U03-01 · Recognise and Name Proper Fractions

**Standards** · CS 3.1 · LS 3.1.1, 3.1.2  
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 2–3 · Activity Book pp. 89–90  
**Learning objective** · Name at least three of four proper fractions shown on equal-part models, including one unit fraction and one with a numerator greater than one.  
**Materials** · Fraction strips, shaded diagram cards and word cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show two strips, one with equal parts and one with unequal parts. Ask which strip can model one third. | Choose the equal-part strip and explain the choice. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model naming 1/5 and 3/5. Trace the whole, count its equal parts and count the selected parts. | Match diagram cards to fraction names, then explain what each number counts. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show models for 1/4, 1/9, 3/7 and 9/10 without labels. Check three correct names, including both kinds of fraction. | Independently name the four fractions aloud or in writing. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what the 7 in 3/7 tells us. | State that the whole has seven equal parts. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils met the naming criterion. Revisit which number counts the whole's equal parts.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U03-02 · Draw and Write Proper Fractions

**Standards** · CS 3.1 · LS 3.1.3, 3.1.4  
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 4–6 · Activity Book pp. 91–92  
**Learning objective** · Represent two given proper fractions on diagrams and write two proper fractions from shaded models, completing at least three items correctly.  
**Materials** · Gridded rectangles, fraction cards and crayons.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Display 2/5 beside a blank five-part strip. Ask how many parts to shade. | Shade two parts and explain the denominator. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model moving in both directions between a written fraction and a diagram. Ask pupils to check that the parts are equal. | Draw or shade fractions for a partner, then write the fraction shown in the partner's model. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 3/5 and 4/9 to model, plus diagrams showing two shaded eighths and seven shaded tenths. Check both directions. | Independently shade the two models and write the two fractions from diagrams. |
| Closure · 5 min | Show a diagram with four of nine parts shaded and the label 9/4. | Correct the label to 4/9 and explain what each number counts. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils completed at least three correctly. Repeat the direction of conversion that caused errors.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U03-03 · Compare Proper Fractions

**Standards** · CS 3.1 · LS 3.1.5  
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 7–9 · Activity Book p. 93  
**Learning objective** · Correctly compare at least three of four fraction pairs and justify one comparison using same-size wholes.  
**Materials** · Equal-length strips partitioned into halves, thirds, fourths, fifths, sixths, sevenths and ninths.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Align 1/3 and 1/6 strips at the same starting point. Ask which piece extends farther. | Point to 1/3 and explain why more equal parts make each part smaller. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Compare fractions with the same denominator and then unit fractions with different denominators. Use 2/3 and 3/4 to show how aligned models can also compare other pairs. | Align strips, point to the longer shaded part and say which fraction is greater or smaller. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 2/7 and 5/7, 1/4 and 1/9, 1/5 and 1/2, and 2/3 and 3/4. Check the comparisons and one correctly aligned model. | Independently compare all four pairs and use strips or drawings to justify one. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why the two whole strips must be the same length. | Explain how equal wholes make the comparison fair. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils compared at least three pairs and justified one. Rebuild the unit-fraction example with pupils who compared denominators alone.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U03-04 · Convert Tenths and Read Decimals

**Standards** · CS 3.2 · LS 3.2.1, 3.2.2, 3.2.5  
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 10–11 · Activity Book pp. 94–95 (shared section reference)  
**Learning objective** · Convert at least three of four tenths fractions to decimals, read two decimals aloud and write one decimal from dictation.  
**Materials** · Tenths strips, fraction-to-decimal cards and mini-whiteboards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Shade one part of a ten-part strip and introduce 1/10 and 0.1 as two names for the same amount. | Read “one tenth” and “zero point one”. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model reading and writing 0.4. Connect the digit after the decimal point to the number of shaded tenths. | Build a tenths model for each fraction card, write the decimal and read it to a partner. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 2/10, 5/10, 7/10 and 9/10. Show 0.2 and 0.7 to read and dictate “zero point six” to write. | Independently convert the four fractions, read the two decimals and write the dictated value. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what the 8 in 0.8 represents. | State that it represents eight tenths. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils met all three checks. Practise reading the point and writing the tenths digit where needed.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U03-05 · Show and Represent Decimals

**Standards** · CS 3.2 · LS 3.2.3, 3.2.4, 3.2.5  
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 11–12 · Activity Book p. 96  
**Learning objective** · Make at least one correct mark for two given decimals on a 0-to-1 number line, shade a model for a decimal and write the decimal shown by a model.  
**Materials** · Number lines divided into tenths and blank ten-part bars.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a strip with six of ten equal parts shaded. Ask where the same amount ends on a matching number line. | Count six intervals from zero and point to 0.6. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model connecting the end of a shaded bar to its number-line mark. Use a mismatched model and ask pupils to repair it. | Make and exchange decimal models, then check the shaded parts against the mark. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask pupils to mark 0.3 and 0.8, shade 0.7 and write the decimal for a bar with five shaded tenths. Check at least one correct mark, the shaded model and the written value. | Independently complete the four tasks and explain one matching pair of representations. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask whether the starting tick is counted as one tenth. | Explain that the first interval from zero represents one tenth. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils met the three representation checks. Use aligned bars and lines to revisit misplaced marks.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U03-06 · Compare Decimals

**Standards** · CS 3.2 · LS 3.2.6  
**References** · Textbook Part 2 p. 13 · Activity Book p. 97  
**Learning objective** · Correctly compare at least three of four pairs of decimals from 0.1 to 0.9 and justify one comparison on a number line.  
**Materials** · Tenths strips, decimal cards and open number lines.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Place 0.2 and 0.5 on a number line. Ask which is farther from zero. | Point to 0.5 and read the two decimals. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model comparing shaded tenths of the same whole, then confirm the result on a line. Ask pupils to switch the order of a pair and revise the comparison. | Match each pair to two models and say which value is greater or smaller. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 0.3 and 0.7, 0.9 and 0.6, 0.4 and 0.1, and 0.5 and 0.8. Check three correct comparisons and a model for one. | Independently compare all four pairs and mark one pair on the line to justify the answer. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how to show the smaller decimal on the number line. | Explain that it is the mark farther left. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils met both checks. Revisit counting intervals where a pupil misplaced a decimal.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U03-07 · Compare Fractions and Decimals

**Standards** · CS 3.3 · LS 3.3.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 2 p. 14 · Activity Book p. 98  
**Learning objective** · Correctly compare at least three of four fraction-and-decimal pairs, including an equal pair, and explain one using a tenths model.  
**Materials** · Tenths strips, a dual-labelled number line and comparison cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a mark labelled 4/10 and ask which decimal belongs at the same point. | Give 0.4 and explain what the two labels have in common. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model comparing 6/10 with 0.8 by aligning equal bars. Add an equal pair and ask pupils to show both labels on one mark. | Convert one member of each pair to the other notation, compare and check on the line. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 2/10 and 0.6, 0.5 and 5/10, 9/10 and 0.7, and 0.3 and 4/10. Check at least three correct comparisons, including the equal pair, and one reason. | Independently write all four comparisons and use a strip or line to explain one. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why a fraction and a decimal can name the same point. | Explain that the two forms can count the same number of tenths. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils met all checks. Match fraction and decimal labels on the same strip to practise equivalence.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U03-08 · Solve Everyday Fraction and Decimal Problems

**Standards** · CS 3.4 · LS 3.4.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 15–17 · Activity Book pp. 99–100  
**Learning objective** · Solve two everyday problems using a diagram and write an answer that interprets the fraction or decimal in context.  
**Materials** · Problem cards, empty strip diagrams and crayons.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a board divided into equal squares and ask what the whole represents. | Identify the whole board and count its equal parts. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model identifying the whole and shading the given parts in a story. For a comparison, align models with the same whole and ask which part is longer. | Draw a model for each practice story and check that the answer responds to the question asked. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask what fraction is painted when three of eight equal squares are painted. Then ask which piece is longer when 0.6 m and 8/10 m are cut from equal one-metre ribbons. Check for 3/8 and the 8/10 m piece. | Independently draw both models, state the fraction and identify the longer ribbon piece with a reason. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what the denominator means in the painted-board story. | Explain that it counts all eight equal squares in the board. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils solved both problems with a model and a valid explanation. Revisit the whole or unit where it was unclear.
