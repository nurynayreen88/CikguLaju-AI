# Mathematics (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 7 Shapes

Six 60-minute RPH for CS 7.1–7.3. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-7-source-check).

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U07-01 · Identify 3-D Shapes From Their Properties

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 77–79 · Activity Book p. 139
**Learning objective** · Identify at least four of five solids from property clues and justify one choice using a face, surface or vertex.
**Materials** · Cube, non-cube cuboid, square-based pyramid, cylinder and cone models, property cards and five individual clues.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a cube and a cylinder. Ask what can be noticed by placing each on its side. | Describe a visible difference and point to a flat or curved surface. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model tracing a face and pointing to a vertex on a solid. Give pairs descriptions of the five solids, using distinguishing properties for each. | Turn and inspect the models, match them to the clues and explain which property separates similar choices. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give clues for six equal square faces, a rectangular box with unequal dimensions, a square base with four triangular faces, two circular flat faces, and one circular base with a point. | Independently identify the cube, cuboid, square-based pyramid, cylinder and cone respectively. Justify one match using a named property. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how to distinguish the cone from the cylinder using their flat faces. | State that the cone has one circular flat face and the cylinder has two. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils identified at least four solids and justified one match. Let pupils who confused two solids compare their models side by side.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U07-02 · Identify the Basic Shapes of 3-D Solids

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.2
**References** · Textbook Part 2 p. 80 · Activity Book p. 140
**Learning objective** · Name the basic shapes and state their counts for at least two of three solids, including one with a curved surface.
**Materials** · Foldable solids, circular lids, detachable cylinder wrap, detachable cone wrap, and shape-and-count cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Press a flat cube face onto paper and trace its outline. Ask what 2-D shape appears. | Identify the square and point to a second square face. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Trace the flat bases of a cylinder and cone. Unwrap the side surfaces to show a rectangle for the cylinder and a sector for the cone. | Trace or unfold models, then record which flat pieces and how many of each make up the solid. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give pictures of a square-based pyramid, cylinder and cone. Ask for the names and counts of the pieces that form each one. | Independently record one square and four triangles, two circles and one rectangle, and one circle and one sector, respectively. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which two models use circles and how many each uses. | Compare the single circle of the cone with the two of the cylinder. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils identified at least two complete sets of pieces, including one curved solid. Reopen a model with pupils who counted only the visible parts.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U07-03 · Recognise Nets of 3-D Shapes

**Standards** · CS 7.1 · LS 7.1.3
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 81–83 · Activity Book pp. 141–142
**Learning objective** · Match at least four of five nets to their solids and identify two different arrangements that fold into cubes.
**Materials** · Five pre-cut nets with tabs where appropriate, solid models, two valid cube nets and a strip of six squares as a comparison.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Open a small cube carton along prepared edges and lay it flat. Ask what remained the same. | Compare the closed carton and the net, noting the same six square faces. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Supply nets for a cube, cuboid, square-based pyramid, cylinder and cone. Ask pupils to predict and fold before matching. Show how a strip of six squares fails to close as a cube. | Fold models, match each net to a solid and compare two working cube arrangements with the strip. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give five net cards for matching. Then offer three cube candidates and ask pupils to select the two that close without overlapping. | Independently match the five cards and mark the two working cube nets, using a fold or a sketch to check. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why counting six squares alone does not prove a cube net will work. | Explain that the squares also need an arrangement that closes into six different faces. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils matched at least four nets and identified both cube nets. Let pupils who chose the strip trace where the faces would overlap or fail to close.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U07-04 · Identify 2-D Shapes From Their Properties

**Standards** · CS 7.2 · LS 7.2.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 84–85 · Activity Book p. 143
**Learning objective** · Identify a square, a non-square rectangle, a triangle and a circle from descriptions and justify one match.
**Materials** · Flat shape cards in varied sizes and orientations, rulers, everyday object pictures and clue slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Rotate a square card and ask whether the number and lengths of its sides changed. | Count the sides again and explain why the rotated card still represents a square. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model reading descriptions about straight sides, corners, equal lengths and a curved boundary. Show a square alongside a rectangle with two long and two short sides. | Compare the shapes, sort the descriptions and explain how the side lengths distinguish the two cards. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give four property descriptions in a new order and ask for one written or oral reason. | Independently identify all four shapes and refer to a matching property for one of them. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which shape has no straight sides and no corners. | Identify the circle and trace its curved boundary. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils identified all four shapes and justified one match. Revisit any rotated example that was identified by its position rather than its properties.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U07-05 · Draw Basic 2-D Shapes

**Standards** · CS 7.2 · LS 7.2.2
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 86–87 · Activity Book p. 144
**Learning objective** · Draw and name a square, a non-square rectangle, a triangle and a circle using suitable tools.
**Materials** · Grid paper, rulers, triangle dot cards, circular stencils and individual drawing sheets.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Draw three connected lines with a gap and ask what is needed to make a closed triangle. | Point to the gap and suggest connecting the final corners. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model drawing a square and rectangle on a grid, joining three points with a ruler and tracing a circle. Show how to check corners and lengths before labelling. | Practise drawing each shape, compare a square with the non-square rectangle and use a partner's checklist to review the drawings. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask for a 3-by-3 square, a 5-by-2 rectangle, a triangle from three provided dots and a circle traced from a stencil. | Independently draw and label the four shapes. Check that every outline is closed. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what a pupil can check to tell the two four-sided drawings apart. | Compare the lengths of adjacent sides on the grid. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils drew and labelled all four shapes. Practise using grid intersections or a stencil with pupils who left gaps or changed a required side length.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U07-06 · Solve Everyday Problems About Shapes

**Standards** · CS 7.3 · LS 7.3.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 88–89 · Activity Book pp. 145–146
**Learning objective** · Solve two everyday design problems and justify each selection with a matching property or set of net pieces.
**Materials** · Reusable packaging models, pattern cards for solid nets, label templates and individual design slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a round lid beside a square box opening. Ask whether the lid matches the opening. | Compare their outlines and explain the mismatch. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model choosing a net for a square-based pyramid display using one square and four triangles. Ask pairs to justify a different package choice using its pieces. | Sketch a plan, match the net to a model and explain why a competing pattern would make another solid. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give two problems: choose a net to make a round tin model with two flat ends, and choose a label with four equal straight sides to cover a square card. | Independently select the net with two matching circles and a rectangle and the square label. Give a reason for each selection. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which clue would distinguish a cone pattern from the tin pattern. | Name the one circle and sector of a cone and compare them with the tin's pieces. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils solved both cases and justified each choice. Revisit matching the words in each design brief to a shape property.
