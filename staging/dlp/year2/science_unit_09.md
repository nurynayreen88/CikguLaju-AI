# Science (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 9 Earth

Eleven 60-minute RPH for CS 9.1 and 9.2. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-9-source-check).

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U09-01 · Identify Natural Sources of Water

**Standards** · CS 9.1 · LS 9.1.1
**References** · Textbook pp. 101–104 · Activity Book pp. 63–64 (identify natural sources)
**Learning objective** · Name five natural sources of water and match at least four to pictures that show their distinctive features.
**Materials** · Illustrated cards for rain, river, lake, sea and spring, labels and individual response slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show rain falling beside a photograph of a lake. Ask how the water appears in each picture. | Describe drops falling and water collected in a place surrounded by land. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use a picture of water emerging from the ground to introduce a spring. Compare a river, lake and sea using visible features and model a five-branch map. | Sort pictures, name each water source and explain one visual clue to a partner. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give five new pictures, including a diagram of groundwater emerging at the surface. Check all five names and the links to the pictures. | Independently name rain, river, lake, sea and spring, then match the labels to the five pictures. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask pupils to distinguish a spring from rain using their origin. | Explain that spring water comes out of the ground and rain falls from clouds. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils named all five sources and made at least four correct matches. Compare two new pictures with pupils who confused a river with a spring.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U09-02 · Observe the Direction of Water Flow

**Standards** · CS 9.1 · LS 9.1.2
**References** · Textbook pp. 105–106 · Activity Book p. 65
**Learning objective** · Observe water in two tilted setups, mark the direction of flow in each and state how the direction relates to height.
**Materials** · Shallow trays, small cups of coloured water, stable blocks, cloths and two-diagram response slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a tray raised at one end. Ask where a small amount of water is likely to collect. | Point to a predicted end and explain the choice using height. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Guide groups to release water near the high end, record what happens and then reverse which end is raised. | Observe and sketch the flow in both setups. Compare where the water collects each time. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give two tray diagrams with opposite slopes. Ask pupils to mark arrows and support their rule with a trial observation. | Independently draw both arrows towards the lower ends and explain that the water changed direction when the high end changed. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask whether water always flows to the right in this investigation. | Refer to the two trials and state that the direction depends on which end is lower. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils drew both arrows correctly and supported the high-to-low rule. Repeat the reverse tilt with pupils who followed a fixed left-to-right direction.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U09-03 · Explain How Rivers and Waterfalls Flow

**Standards** · CS 9.1 · LS 9.1.3
**References** · Textbook p. 107 · Activity Book pp. 66–67
**Learning objective** · Use two landscape representations to generalise that rivers and waterfalls flow from higher to lower places.
**Materials** · Waterfall photograph, river landscape diagram with marked heights, two new sketch cards and pencils.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a waterfall photograph and ask where the water starts and lands. | Identify the top and the pool below and point to the direction of movement. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Compare the photograph with a river diagram showing a hillside and a lowland. Turn the diagram so the downhill path points the opposite way on the page. | Trace the water in each representation and explain what stays the same when the diagram is turned. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Provide a new waterfall sketch and a river sketch with high and low points. Check both arrows and a sentence supported by the examples. | Independently draw both arrows from higher to lower ground and state the common direction of natural water flow. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which clue is more useful, the side of the page or the height of the land. | Choose the land height and justify it with one example. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils explained the same high-to-low pattern in both examples. Revisit the height marks with pupils who followed the layout of the page.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U09-04 · Sequence the Natural Water Cycle

**Standards** · CS 9.1 · LS 9.1.4
**References** · Textbook pp. 108–109 · Activity Book p. 68
**Learning objective** · Place four water-cycle stages in sequence and draw the return arrow to show that the cycle continues.
**Materials** · Cards for surface water, water vapour, clouds and rain, arrow cards and blank cycle mats.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a picture of a puddle becoming smaller on a sunny day. Ask what may happen to some of the water. | Suggest that some water changes to vapour and mixes with the air. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model arranging surface water, water vapour, clouds and rain. Explain that vapour cools into tiny droplets that form clouds and that rain returns water to rivers and seas. | Arrange the cards, link them with arrows and tell a partner how the final stage leads back to the first. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Provide a shuffled four-stage set. Check the order and direction of each arrow, including the return from rain to surface water. | Independently order and label the four stages and complete the circular path. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask whether the cycle has to start with the sea in every diagram. | Point to a different starting stage and trace the same order around the cycle. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils ordered the stages and showed the return path correctly. Use an arrow mat with pupils who stopped the cycle at rain.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U09-05 · Explain Observations About the Water Cycle

**Standards** · CS 9.1 · LS 9.1.5
**References** · Textbook pp. 108–109 · Activity Book p. 69
**Learning objective** · Describe where droplets form and where they fall in a model, then link those observations to clouds and rain in the water cycle.
**Materials** · Teacher-prepared clear bowl with comfortably warm water, clear cover cooled on top with an ice pack, response sheets.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a dry clear cover and ask pupils to predict where water might appear when it covers the bowl. | Make a prediction and identify the surface they will observe. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Keep the ice pack outside the covered bowl and point out droplets forming underneath. Observe droplets gathering and falling, then discuss how the cooled cover represents cooling in the air. | Record where droplets first appeared and where they moved. Explain how the model represents cloud droplets and rain. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask for a labelled sketch, short caption or oral response giving both observations and their links to the natural cycle. | Independently explain that vapour cooled into droplets on the underside and that larger drops fell, like rain returning water to the surface. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why the class used a dry cover at the start. | Explain that it helps establish that the droplets formed during the demonstration. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils communicated both observations and linked them to the cycle. Compare the underside and top of the cover with pupils who attributed the droplets to the ice pack leaking.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U09-06 · Show That Air Is Around Us

**Standards** · CS 9.2 · LS 9.2.1
**References** · Textbook pp. 110–111 · Activity Book p. 70
**Learning objective** · State that air surrounds us and describe two observations that show air can occupy space in familiar places.
**Materials** · Clear cups, water basin, dry tissue, small clear bag and observation slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Hold up a limp clear bag, sweep it through the room and close it. Ask what made it expand. | Observe the change and suggest that air entered the bag. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Put tissue in the top of an inverted cup and lower it straight into water. Tilt the cup in a second trial so bubbles escape and water enters. | Compare both trials, observe the bubbles and describe how the air affected the water entering the cup. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask each pupil to state where the air came from in the bag and what the cup experiment showed. Check both explanations against the observations. | Independently state that air is around us, then explain that it filled the bag and occupied space in the inverted cup. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what the bubbles showed when the cup was tilted. | Explain that some air left the cup and moved through the water. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils stated that air surrounds us and described both observations. Repeat the two cup positions with pupils who thought the cup held nothing.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U09-07 · Name Gases Found in Air

**Standards** · CS 9.2 · LS 9.2.2
**References** · Textbook pp. 110–111 · Activity Book p. 71
**Learning objective** · State that air is a mixture of gases, name oxygen and carbon dioxide, and identify oxygen as a gas people need for breathing.
**Materials** · Air-mixture diagram, gas word cards, breathing illustration and individual response slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a picture of a child breathing outdoors. Ask whether air is made of one gas or several. | Share a prediction and name any gas they already know. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use a diagram that labels oxygen and carbon dioxide alongside other gases. Explain that oxygen is needed for breathing and that the air we breathe out still contains several gases. | Place the two gas labels in the air diagram and explain why the names do not describe all of the air. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask pupils to complete a short air-mixture statement, name two example gases and identify the gas people need for breathing. | Independently name oxygen and carbon dioxide, state that air contains several gases and select oxygen for breathing. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask whether a bag filled with ordinary air contains only oxygen. | Explain that oxygen is one of several gases in the bag. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils named both gases, explained that air is a mixture and identified oxygen for breathing. Revisit the labelled diagram with pupils who treated air and oxygen as the same thing.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U09-08 · Describe Moving Air as Wind

**Standards** · CS 9.2 · LS 9.2.3
**References** · Textbook pp. 112–113 · Activity Book p. 73
**Learning objective** · Identify wind as moving air and give two observable signs that air moved during a classroom demonstration.
**Materials** · Card fans, paper streamers, stands and individual comparison slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a hanging streamer before and after waving a card fan. Ask what changed without touching the streamer. | Describe the movement and suggest what reached the paper. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Guide pupils to alternate between holding the fan still and waving it from a marked distance. Ask them to notice both the streamer and the air they feel on a hand. | Compare the two conditions and record whether the paper moved and whether they felt air moving. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask each pupil to describe both signs and state what wind means. Check the link between the moving air and each observation. | Independently state that wind is moving air, then explain that they felt it and saw it move the streamer. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what pupils observed when the fan was held still. | Explain that the streamer stopped moving after the airflow from the fan stopped. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils defined wind and gave both signs of moving air. Repeat the still and moving conditions with pupils who named only the fan.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U09-09 · Explain How Wind Affects Daily Life

**Standards** · CS 9.2 · LS 9.2.4
**References** · Textbook pp. 112–113, 115–116 · Activity Book pp. 74–75
**Learning objective** · Give two useful effects of wind and one harmful effect of strong wind, explaining what changes in each case.
**Materials** · Pictures of laundry, a sailing boat, a spinning windmill and strong wind near trees, with individual scenario cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show clothes on a line and ask how a breeze can affect them. | Suggest an effect and point to a clue in the picture. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Compare a helpful breeze with strong winds in several settings. Model a sentence linking moving air to a visible change. | Sort situations by effect and explain how air can move a sail or help dry laundry, and how strong wind can damage property. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give pictures of a sailboat, damp cloth and trees beside a roof during strong wind. Check two useful effects and one harmful effect with a reason for each. | Independently explain that wind pushes the sail, can help cloth dry faster and can cause branches or loose items to damage the roof. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how the same moving air can be useful in one situation and harmful in another. | Compare a gentle breeze and a strong wind using one example of each. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils gave two useful effects and one harmful effect with clear explanations. Use pictures to help pupils link each effect to the object that changes.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U09-10 · Build a Model Sailboat

**Standards** · CS 9.2 · LS 9.2.5
**References** · Textbook p. 114 (moving-air design) · Activity Book p. 72 (sailboat); p. 76 (windmill extension)
**Learning objective** · Build a model boat with a sail, demonstrate how moving air propels it and describe one change that improves its movement or stability.
**Materials** · Sponge bases, paper sails, blunt craft sticks, tape, shallow water trays and card fans.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show an upright paper sail and ask what a breeze might do to it. | Predict how the sail could help move a floating model. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Demonstrate securing a paper sail to a sponge base. Give pairs time to test it first with the fan still, then with moving air. Ask them to revise one feature if the boat tips or turns. | Sketch and build a boat, test both conditions and refine the position of the sail or the balance of the base. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask each pupil to show the model moving and explain one design change or trial adjustment. Check the demonstration and the reason given. | Independently operate the model, point to where the airflow meets the sail and describe how the change affected the boat. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what is likely to happen after the fan stops and the model slows. | Explain that the moving air no longer pushes the sail. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils demonstrated a working sailboat and explained one adjustment. Help pupils with unstable designs move the sail closer to the centre of the base.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U09-11 · Explain Observations About Air

**Standards** · CS 9.2 · LS 9.2.6
**References** · Textbook pp. 110, 112, 114–116 · Activity Book p. 72 (review of the model)
**Learning objective** · Communicate two observations about air and link each to evidence from the trapped-air or sailboat activity.
**Materials** · Class records for the cup and sailboat, demonstration kits, picture prompts and individual response slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a cup releasing bubbles and ask which part of the description is an observation. | Describe the visible bubbles before suggesting that air escaped. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Revisit the cup tilted under water and the sailboat with and without a moving fan. Model a short explanation supported by a specific observation. | Compare the records, identify what they saw and discuss what each observation shows about air. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask pupils to present two observation–explanation pairs by sketching, writing or speaking. Check that one concerns trapped air and the other concerns moving air. | Independently explain how bubbles showed air leaving the cup and how the boat's movement showed air pushing the sail. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask pupils to point to the part of their answer that provides evidence. | Identify a detail that supports one explanation. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils gave two accurate observation–explanation pairs. Revisit the original demonstration with pupils who gave an explanation without naming the evidence.
