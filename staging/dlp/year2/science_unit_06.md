# Science (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 6 Light and Dark

Six 60-minute RPH for CS 6.1. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-6-source-check).

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U06-01 · Identify Sources of Light

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.1
**References** · Textbook pp. 61–63 · Activity Book p. 37
**Learning objective** · Identify three sources of light and explain why a mirror in the same scene is a reflector.
**Materials** · Pictures of the Sun and a lit candle, a battery torch, mirror, white card and four illustrated response prompts.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a battery torch switched off and on. Ask when it gives light. | Compare the two states and identify the lit torch as a source. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use the torch and mirror to make a patch of light on a card. Switch the torch off and ask what changes. Give pairs scene cards to sort. | Observe that the patch disappears. Sort the Sun, a lit candle, a lit torch and a mirror by whether each gives out its own light. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show a new illustration with the Sun, a glowing lamp, a candle flame and a mirror. Check all three choices and the explanation about the mirror. | Independently mark the three sources and explain that the mirror sends light from another source towards the viewer. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which of the three sources can light an outdoor playground during the day. | Identify the Sun and describe one sign that its light reaches the playground. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils identified all three sources and explained the role of the mirror. Repeat the torch and mirror demonstration with pupils who confused reflection with emission.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U06-02 · Compare Activities in Light and Dark

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.2
**References** · Textbook pp. 64–65 · Activity Book pp. 38–40
**Learning objective** · Compare success at identifying symbols in light and dark and give one conclusion supported by the recorded observations.
**Materials** · Identical enclosed viewing boxes with the same arrangement of four large symbols, torch mounted on one box, second symbol set, recording slips and a picture of a reader.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a picture of someone trying to read a sign when a light is off. Ask pupils what would help. | Suggest turning on a light and predict how it will affect reading. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use a lit and an unlit box while the classroom remains lit. Give the same viewing time and symbol positions in both conditions. Rotate which box is viewed first and use a second equivalent symbol set to reduce recall. | Count how many symbols they can identify correctly in each condition and record both observations. Compare their results with a partner. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a sample result of four correct in the lit box and one in the unlit box. Ask each pupil to interpret the evidence and apply it to reading the sign. | Independently identify the easier condition, cite both results and explain why the sign is easier to read when lit. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why both viewing times should match. | Explain that keeping the time the same makes the comparison about the light. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils compared both observations and explained the effect of light. Practise turning paired results into a comparison sentence with pupils who gave only one result.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U06-03 · Explain How a Shadow Forms

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.3
**References** · Textbook pp. 66–67 · Activity Book p. 41
**Learning objective** · Make a shadow on a screen and identify the correct arrangement in two contrasting diagrams, explaining how the shadow forms.
**Materials** · LED torch, card silhouette with a stand, white card screen, marked mat and individual diagram slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Shine a torch onto a screen and ask where to place a card to darken part of the screen. | Predict a position for the card and explain their choice. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Keep the screen and torch fixed. Ask pairs to put the card between them, then off to one side. Keep the torch aimed at the screen. | Test both positions, draw the arrangement and record whether a shadow appears on the screen. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give diagrams with the card in the beam and to the side. Ask pupils to mark the shadow position and explain it. | Independently identify the in-beam arrangement, shade the region behind the card on the screen and explain that the card blocks light. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what happens to the shadow when the card is removed. | Predict that the patch disappears as light reaches the screen again. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils located the shadow and explained that light was blocked. Rebuild the arrangement with pupils who placed the shadow on the torch side of the card.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U06-04 · Investigate the Clarity of Shadows

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.4
**References** · Textbook pp. 67–68 · Activity Book p. 42
**Learning objective** · Record the shadow clarity of three materials in a fair comparison and support a conclusion with at least two observations.
**Materials** · Same-sized shapes cut from card, tracing paper and clear acetate, clips, LED torch, white screen and marked positions.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Hold a card shape and an acetate shape of the same size against the same light. Ask what might appear on the screen. | Predict which material will make a clearer shadow and give a reason. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Mark the positions for the torch, material and screen. Use the same lighting and orientation for all three trials. Model recording clear, less clear or no discernible shadow. | Take turns changing only the material. Observe the screen, record results for all three and compare the sharpness or visibility of the shadows. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask for an individual record and a comparison sentence naming two materials and their observed outcomes. Accept faint edges on clear acetate when reported accurately. | Independently name the clearest shadow, compare it with another trial and explain the difference in terms of how much light passed through. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which material would best make a clearly visible shadow puppet. | Choose a material based on the recorded result. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils recorded three observations and used two in their explanation. Repeat the comparison for pupils who changed the torch or object position during trials.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U06-05 · Create a Shadow Game

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.5
**References** · Textbook pp. 69–71 · Activity Book pp. 43–44
**Learning objective** · Create and demonstrate a guessing game with two distinct shadows and explain one change that made a shadow easier to recognise.
**Materials** · LED torches, white screens, opaque card, blunt craft sticks, clips, two sample silhouette cards and an individual game slip.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show the shadow of a card bird behind a screen. Ask what clues make it recognisable. | Name a feature such as the beak or wings. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Demonstrate fixing a light and placing a silhouette between the light and the screen. Give pairs time to design two silhouettes, test them and refine a weak outline. | Make two different shapes. Show them in turn to a partner and revise the outline or angle if the partner cannot recognise the subject. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Watch each pupil operate the game and show two distinct shadows. Ask the pupil to describe one change made after the trial. | Independently run both clues, then draw or state the change and how it helped someone recognise a shape. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why card silhouettes usually work well for this game. | Link the visible outline to the card blocking light. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils demonstrated two shadows and explained an improvement. Support pupils who need a clearer silhouette by testing it at a fixed position before changing the design.

## DLP-SCI-Y2-U06-06 · Explain Observations About Light and Dark

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.6
**References** · Textbook pp. 69, 71–72 · Activity Book pp. 43–44 (review of earlier trials)
**Learning objective** · Communicate two observations about light and dark and explain each using a labelled sketch, written caption or oral response.
**Materials** · Two class observation records from the viewing-box and shadow trials, example sketch with arrows and individual response cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a photograph of a shadow from the previous lesson and ask what it shows and what caused it. | Give one observation and then a separate explanation. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Revisit the lighted and dark viewing boxes and a screen with an opaque shape in the beam. Model a short explanation backed by a visible result. | Compare the two demonstrations and discuss which observation supports each explanation. Practise labelling the source, object and shadow. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask each pupil to explain why symbols were easier to identify in the lit box and how the shadow appeared on the screen. Check each response against the observed evidence. | Independently present two observation–explanation pairs in their chosen format. Refer to the light in the viewing box and the blocked beam in the shadow trial. |
| Closure · 5 min | Invite a pupil to share one label or word that made the explanation clearer. | Share it and identify the observation it explains. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils communicated two accurate observations with explanations. Revisit the relevant trial with pupils whose responses reported an outcome without its cause.
