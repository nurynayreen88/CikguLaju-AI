# Mathematics (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 6 Length, Mass, and Volume of Liquid

Ten 60-minute RPH for CS 6.1–6.4. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-6-source-check).

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U06-01 · Recognise Centimetres and Metres

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 54–55 · Activity Book p. 125
**Learning objective** · Read one marked graduation on each length tool and choose and write the suitable unit for two illustrated objects.
**Materials** · Centimetre rulers, metre ruler or measuring tape, marked graduation cards and picture slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Hold a ruler next to a crayon and a measuring tape next to a door. Ask why two tools are useful. | Compare their sizes and predict which will be easier to use in each case. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Point out the zero and the numbered centimetre marks on a ruler. Show metre marks on a measuring tape and match both unit names to their symbols. | Locate the zero and marked graduations on each tool, read selected marks and pair centimetres with cm and metres with m. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show marks at 14 on a centimetre ruler and 3 at a whole-metre mark. Add pictures of a key and a classroom wall. | Independently write 14 cm and 3 m for the marked readings and choose cm for the key and m for the wall. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask pupils to name another short object and another longer distance. | Give a plausible example for each unit. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils read both marks and chose both suitable units. Revisit the labels on each tool with pupils who used the same symbol for both.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U06-02 · Measure Length and Draw Lines

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.2
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 56–58 · Activity Book pp. 126–127
**Learning objective** · Measure a card strip to the nearest centimetre, mark a distance of 1 m and draw a straight line of a specified length.
**Materials** · Centimetre rulers, measuring tapes, teacher-prepared 9 cm strips, masking tape and blank response slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a strip whose end is aligned with the ruler's 1 cm mark. Ask what must be changed before reading the other end. | Move the first end to the zero mark and explain the starting position. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model measuring a strip with both ends aligned to the scale and drawing a 6 cm line using marked endpoints. Show how to keep a measuring tape straight along a floor lane. | Measure two strips, draw a 6 cm line and take turns marking a 1 m distance with tape. Check a partner's zero and endpoint. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Provide a 9 cm strip and a new starting dot. Ask pupils to measure the strip, draw a 7 cm line and demonstrate the endpoint of 1 m at the station. | Independently record 9 cm, draw a 7 cm line from the dot and indicate the correct point on the floor lane. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why the ruler must stay still while a line is drawn. | Explain how a moving ruler can change the position or length of the line. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils measured, marked and drew accurately. Repeat zero alignment with pupils whose answer was one graduation too large.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U06-03 · Estimate and Check Length

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.3
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 59–60 · Activity Book p. 128
**Learning objective** · Use a benchmark to estimate two lengths, then measure them and state whether each estimate is shorter, longer or equal.
**Materials** · A 10 cm benchmark strip, metre ruler, a classroom object suitable for cm, a distance suitable for m and recording tables.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show the 10 cm benchmark next to a longer strip. Ask whether the strip is closer to 10 cm or twice that length. | Use the benchmark to give and justify a rough estimate. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model recording an estimate before measuring. Set up a short-object station and a longer-distance station with clear endpoints. | Choose cm or m, use the appropriate benchmark and record their estimates. Measure with a ruler or tape and compare with the estimates. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give each pupil two new lengths and ask for the estimate, actual reading and comparison for each. Check the measured values against the classroom setup. | Independently record both comparisons with units and describe one benchmark used. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how the 10 cm strip could help estimate an object's length without measuring it exactly. | Explain how to compare the object with the known length mentally. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils completed both estimate–measure comparisons with the correct units. Guide pupils who changed their original estimates to record both values separately.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U06-04 · Recognise Grams and Kilograms

**Standards** · CS 6.2 · LS 6.2.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 61–62 · Activity Book p. 129
**Learning objective** · Read a labelled scale in grams and a labelled scale in kilograms and select a suitable unit for two pictured objects.
**Materials** · Gram and kilogram scales or dial models, labelled packets, a small eraser, a bag of books and response cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a small eraser and a bag of books. Ask which would usually be described in grams. | Compare the objects and give a reason for their choice. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Show a gram scale and a kilogram scale. Compare the printed symbols and model reading the numbered graduation before saying the unit. | Match g to grams and kg to kilograms, then read labelled dial cards and match them to suitable examples. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show dial readings at 300 on a g scale and 4 on a kg scale. Ask for suitable units for an eraser and a full schoolbag. | Independently write 300 g and 4 kg, then choose g for the eraser and kg for the schoolbag. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how to tell whether a displayed 4 is in grams or kilograms. | Point to the printed symbol on the scale. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils read the two scales and chose both suitable units. Revisit the scale labels with pupils who wrote g and kg interchangeably.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U06-05 · Weigh Objects

**Standards** · CS 6.2 · LS 6.2.2
**References** · Textbook Part 2 p. 63 · Activity Book p. 130
**Learning objective** · Check that a scale starts at zero, weigh two objects and record their readings with suitable units.
**Materials** · Stable scales with appropriate capacity and clearly marked graduations, a small beanbag, a bag of books and a mass table.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show the pointer on an empty scale. Ask what must be checked before adding an object. | Check that the pointer is on zero and suggest resetting it if needed. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model placing an object on the centre of a scale and waiting for the reading to settle. Show how to count graduations when numbers are spaced apart. | Choose a suitable scale, take turns weighing a light object and a heavier one, then compare readings with a partner. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give pupils an individual turn at both stations. Check zero, the reading and the written unit against the measured classroom values. | Independently weigh both objects and record the readings in g or kg as displayed. |
| Closure · 5 min | Show a large empty container and a small solid object. Ask whether size alone determines mass. | Explain that both must be weighed to compare their mass reliably. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils checked zero and recorded two valid readings. Revisit the meaning of each graduation with pupils whose units were correct but readings differed.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U06-06 · Estimate and Check Mass

**Standards** · CS 6.2 · LS 6.2.3
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 64–65 · Activity Book p. 131
**Learning objective** · Estimate two masses using a reference object, check both estimates on scales and describe the difference for each.
**Materials** · A labelled 100 g reference and 1 kg reference, two suitable test objects, gram and kilogram scales, and a recording table.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Let pupils compare a test object with the 100 g reference in their hands. Ask whether it feels lighter, heavier or similar. | Give a rough comparison using the known mass. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model writing an estimate before weighing. Guide pupils to choose a reference near the expected mass and use a scale with suitable capacity. | Compare each object with a reference, estimate its mass with a unit and weigh it to obtain an actual reading. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Present two fresh objects, one for each scale. Check that each pupil records the estimate, reading and direction of the difference. | Independently compare their estimates with the measured masses and explain one estimate using the chosen reference. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask which reference would be more useful for estimating a bag of books. | Choose the 1 kg reference and explain why it is closer in mass. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils made two estimates and compared each with an actual reading. Revisit choosing a benchmark with pupils whose estimates used an unsuitable unit.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U06-07 · Recognise Millilitres and Litres

**Standards** · CS 6.3 · LS 6.3.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 66–67 · Activity Book p. 132
**Learning objective** · Read a marked graduation in millilitres and one in litres and match each unit to a plausible everyday quantity.
**Materials** · Small graduated cylinder, large marked jug, bottle and carton labels, and picture slips with clear graduations.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a small juice carton and a large water bottle. Ask pupils to find the quantity on each label. | Point to each quantity and notice that the labels use different units. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Show the graduations and symbols mℓ and ℓ on two containers. Model reading a marked line by saying both the value and the unit. | Match unit names to symbols and use picture cards to read labelled graduations and match them to everyday amounts. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show marks at 250 on an mℓ scale and 2 on an ℓ scale. Ask for the likely units for a small juice carton and a large water container. | Independently record 250 mℓ and 2 ℓ, then select the suitable units for both objects. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what information the number 250 is missing when written on its own. | Name the unit needed to communicate the amount. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils read both graduations and chose both units. Revisit the symbols with pupils who treated the quantities as interchangeable.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U06-08 · Measure Liquid Volume

**Standards** · CS 6.3 · LS 6.3.2
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 68–69 · Activity Book p. 133
**Learning objective** · Measure and record 150 mℓ and 1 ℓ using suitable vessels and read both levels at eye height.
**Materials** · Water, a graduated plastic jug with a 150 mℓ mark, a larger jug with a 1 ℓ mark, trays and recording slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show how a water level appears from above and from eye height. Ask which position gives a dependable reading. | Compare both views and choose eye height. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Place the vessel on a level surface and show how to add water slowly near the required mark. Use small and large vessels for the respective volumes. | Select a suitable vessel, pour water to a set level, wait for it to settle and read the scale at eye height. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask pupils to demonstrate 150 mℓ and 1 ℓ and check the vessel, reading position, level and written units. | Independently measure the two volumes and record each reading with its unit. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why a small cylinder is a poor choice for measuring a whole litre in one pour. | Explain that it lacks enough capacity. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils measured both volumes and recorded them with suitable units. Reteach level placement and eye height for pupils who read from above.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U06-09 · Estimate and Check Liquid Volume

**Standards** · CS 6.3 · LS 6.3.3
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 70–71 · Activity Book p. 134
**Learning objective** · Use benchmarks to estimate two liquid volumes, measure both and compare each estimate with the actual reading.
**Materials** · Clear benchmark vessels marked at 250 mℓ and 1 ℓ, two different containers with teacher-prepared water amounts, graduated jugs, trays and recording cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a narrow container and a wide container. Ask whether the taller water level always indicates more liquid. | Make a prediction and suggest measuring to check it. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Show a known 250 mℓ amount and a 1 ℓ amount. Model choosing the nearer reference before estimating and pouring into a measuring vessel to check. | Estimate the contents of the two containers, then pour and read the measuring scale. Record estimates and readings in separate columns. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Provide two new prepared amounts in different vessels. Check each estimate, measured value, unit and comparison without requiring exact predictions. | Independently estimate and measure both quantities and state whether each estimate was lower, higher or equal. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why a known reference helps when the containers have different shapes. | Explain that liquid height alone gives an incomplete comparison. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils estimated, measured and compared both volumes. Guide pupils who chose the taller vessel automatically to test equal volumes in two different shapes.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U06-10 · Solve Everyday Measurement Problems

**Standards** · CS 6.4 · LS 6.4.1
**References** · Textbook Part 2 pp. 72–75 · Activity Book pp. 135–136
**Learning objective** · Solve three everyday problems covering length, mass and liquid volume and justify each answer with a comparison or calculation in the given unit.
**Materials** · Height and clearance cards, mass cards, capacity pictures and individual response slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a 90 cm shelf opening and a box that is 80 cm high. Ask how to decide whether the box fits. | Compare 80 cm with 90 cm and explain that the box fits with room to spare. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model finding the known quantity and the requested decision in a shopping or classroom story. Compare capacities using the same units and ask pupils to show which quantity is bigger. | Mark the quantities, choose a comparison or calculation and explain the result using a sketch or number sentence. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give three cases: a 105 cm stand in a 100 cm space, a 350 g pack and a 500 g pack asking for the difference, and 600 mℓ of water with a 500 mℓ jug or a 750 mℓ jug. | Independently state that the stand is 5 cm too tall, calculate a 150 g difference and choose the 750 mℓ jug because it can hold 600 mℓ. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask pupils to show which fact helped them choose the jug. | Point to the capacity and compare it with the amount to be poured. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils solved all three problems and explained each decision in the given unit. Revisit identifying the quantity and the question with pupils who compared the wrong values.
