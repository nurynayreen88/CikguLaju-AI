# Mathematics (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 1 Numbers up to 1,000

These twelve CikguLaju lesson plans cover whole numbers up to 1,000 (CS 1.1–1.8) in the KSSR (Semakan 2017) English-edition DSKP. Each plan is 60 minutes. The codes identify staging records. See the [source mapping](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-unit-1-source-check).

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-01 · Recognise Numbers

**Standards** · CS 1.1 · LS 1.1.1(i), (ii), (iii)  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 2–5 · Activity Book pp. 1–2  
**Learning objective** · Say one number shown as a numeral and read one number shown in words, then match at least four of five numerals to their word forms.  
**Materials** · Number and word cards, including pairs such as 306 and 360.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show 306 and 360. Ask what changes when the zero moves. | Read the two numbers and compare what they hear. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model reading a three-digit number in place-value order. Give pairs shuffled numeral and word cards. | Match the cards. Read the written form and say the number on the numeral card to verify each match. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show 520 and the words “seven hundred and two” as separate oral prompts. Check the five independent matches for 217, 260, 402, 420 and 618. | Refer to Activity Book pp. 1–2 if available. Match the five numbers to shuffled word cards independently and read both oral prompts to the teacher. |
| Closure · 5 min | Show 614 and 641 and ask which numeral matches “six hundred and fourteen”. | Choose a numeral and say it aloud. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils read both formats and matched at least four pairs. Practise numbers with a zero in tens or ones where needed.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-02 · Write Numbers

**Standards** · CS 1.2 · LS 1.2.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 6–7 · Activity Book pp. 3–4  
**Learning objective** · Write at least four of five numbers correctly, including one conversion in each direction between numerals and words.  
**Materials** · Mini-whiteboards, place-value mats and a five-item response slip.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Say “four hundred and seven”. Show 407 and 470 and ask which one matches. | Choose a numeral and explain what the zero represents. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use a hundreds-tens-ones mat to model 805, then show how to write it in words. Give pairs alternating numeral and word prompts. | Write each number in the other form and check a partner's response against the mat. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give “two hundred and eight” and “six hundred and thirty” for pupils to write in numerals, and 315, 470 and 906 to write in words. Note recurring zero or digit-order errors. | Refer to Activity Book pp. 3–4 if available. Complete the five conversions independently. |
| Closure · 5 min | Present one swapped-digit answer and ask pupils to correct it. | Write the correct answer and explain the change. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils scored at least four of five with success in both directions. Reteach the conversion direction that caused errors.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-03 · Show and Match Number Values

**Standards** · CS 1.1 · LS 1.1.2(i), (ii)  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 8–12 · Activity Book pp. 5–6  
**Learning objective** · Build two numbers using base-ten blocks and correctly match two block pictures to their numerals.  
**Materials** · Base-ten blocks, block pictures and numeral cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a unit cube, a ten rod and a hundred flat. Ask what quantity each represents. | Name each quantity and explain how ten unit cubes form a ten. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model 284 with blocks. Provide a mixture of numeral cards and pictures, including numbers with zero tens or ones. | Build a number from a numeral, then switch roles and match a pictured group to its numeral. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask pupils to build 307 and 460. Show pictures of two flats, five rods and one cube, and of six flats and two cubes. Check the two builds and the matches to 251 and 602. | Refer to Activity Book pp. 5–6 if available. Build and match the four items independently. |
| Closure · 5 min | Show a group with four hundred flats and three unit cubes. | Hold up 403 and explain why no ten rods are needed. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils completed all four items. Rebuild a zero-place example with pupils who added blocks to fill an empty column.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-04 · Place Value and Digit Value

**Standards** · CS 1.4 · LS 1.4.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 13–15 · Activity Book pp. 7–9  
**Learning objective** · State both the place and digit value of a marked digit in at least three of four examples.  
**Materials** · Place-value mats, digit cards and mini-whiteboards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show 242. Point to the two 2s and ask what each represents. | Describe the difference between the value of the first and last 2. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model “The place is tens. The digit value is 40.” Move a digit across columns and ask pupils to describe its new value. | Use a mat to change the digit positions, then give the place and digit value for a partner's marked digit. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Mark 3 in 318, 2 in 425, 9 in 619 and 0 in 708. Count an item correct when both the place and digit value are correct. | Refer to Activity Book pp. 7–9 if available. Write both answers for each marked digit independently. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask for the place and digit value of 0 in 503. | State “tens” and “zero” and point to the tens column. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils answered at least three items correctly. Use a place-value mat to distinguish the column name from the amount represented.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-05 · Partition Numbers

**Standards** · CS 1.4 · LS 1.4.2  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 16–17 · Activity Book pp. 10–11  
**Learning objective** · Partition two of three numbers correctly in both forms, using place names and expanded sums.  
**Materials** · Digit cards, place-value mats and response slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Display 300 + 50 + 8 and ask pupils to combine the parts. | Build 358 and name the three groups. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model 705 as seven hundreds, zero tens and five ones, then as 700 + 5. Use the mat to show why both forms represent the same number. | Partition three-digit numbers in both forms. Check each answer by recombining its parts. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 436, 802 and 570. Check that the values add to the original number and that the two forms agree. | Refer to Activity Book pp. 10–11 if available. Write both partitions for each number independently. |
| Closure · 5 min | Show 650 as 600 + 5 and ask pupils to repair the error. | Write 600 + 50 and explain the value of 5 in the tens place. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils wrote both forms for at least two numbers. Provide a place-value mat to pupils who used a digit in place of its value.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-06 · Compare Numbers

**Standards** · CS 1.1 · LS 1.1.2(iii)  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 18–21 · Activity Book pp. 12–13  
**Learning objective** · Correctly compare at least three of four pairs of numbers up to 1,000 and explain one comparison using place value.  
**Materials** · Numeral cards, hundreds-tens-ones mats and a number line.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show 391 and 319. Ask which number is greater and which column settles it. | Compare from the hundreds column and explain why the tens decide. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model comparing from the highest place. Include pairs with equal hundreds and equal tens. Extend the mat to thousands when comparing with 1,000 and check one comparison on a number line. | Arrange cards from each pair, tell a partner which number is greater and name the first place that differs. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give pairs 327 and 372, 608 and 604, 95 and 205, and 999 and 1,000. Check four comparisons and ask pupils to justify one using the deciding place. | Refer to Activity Book pp. 12–13 if available. Independently write four “greater than” or “less than” comparisons and explain one. |
| Closure · 5 min | Present 599 and 600 and ask what decides the comparison. | Choose 600 and explain that six hundreds exceed five hundreds. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils compared at least three pairs and explained one. Revisit highest-place comparisons with pupils who relied on the ones digit.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-07 · Arrange Numbers

**Standards** · CS 1.1 · LS 1.1.2(iv)  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 22–23 · Activity Book pp. 14–15  
**Learning objective** · Arrange four pictured quantities correctly from least to greatest and from greatest to least.  
**Materials** · Cards showing groups of hundred flats, ten rods and unit cubes, with separate numeral labels.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show three pictured collections and ask which has the smallest quantity. | Compare the groups and point to the smallest. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model how to label the collections, find the smallest and compare the two closest values. Reverse the row and name both directions. | Match numerals to pictured collections, then build two rows in opposite orders and read each row aloud. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give four pictured quantities: 209, 290, 219 and 201. Check both full rows and ask which two are closest to 209 in the ascending row. | Arrange the picture cards independently in both orders and record the two numeral sequences. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what happens to the smallest group when the row is reversed. | Identify the new position and state whether the row increases or decreases. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils ordered all four collections in both directions. Revisit the first differing place when two values were swapped.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-08 · Count Numbers and Complete Sequences

**Standards** · CS 1.3 · LS 1.3.1, 1.3.2  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 24–28 · Activity Book pp. 16–18  
**Learning objective** · Count forward in tens and backward in hundreds for three steps each, and fill at least three of four missing terms in given sequences.  
**Materials** · Number lines, ten rods, hundred flats and sequence slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Start at 190 and ask what comes next when one ten is added. | Show one ten rod and say 200. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model stepping forward in tens from 470 and back in hundreds from 920. Show how an empty position represents a whole step. | Move counters or mark jumps on a number line, say each landing number and explain one missing term to a partner. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Listen to three steps from 280 in tens and three from 840 back in hundreds. Check blanks in 197, __, 199, __ and 960, __, 760, __. | Count both short runs and fill the four blanks independently. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how to check a missing term when a number on either side is given. | Show a jump from one side and check it against the other. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils counted both runs and filled at least three blanks. Practise counting across a hundred boundary as needed.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-09 · Estimate Numbers

**Standards** · CS 1.5 · LS 1.5.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 29–31 · Activity Book pp. 19–20  
**Learning objective** · Give a reasonable estimate for two groups of objects and justify each estimate using a known reference quantity.  
**Materials** · Equal-size grid cards, a reference card with 20 dots, and target cards with 37 and 62 dots arranged at similar spacing.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Display a card with 20 dots and a card with a larger group. Ask for an estimate using the known group. | Compare the areas covered and suggest how many groups of 20 might fit. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model using one section of a grid as a reference and combining approximate groups. Count one practice card afterwards to check the estimate. | Estimate in pairs using a phrase such as “about two groups of 20”. Count afterwards and discuss how close the estimate was. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show the 37-dot and 62-dot cards. Accept 30–50 and 50–70 respectively for these cards when pupils explain each estimate using the 20-dot reference. | Independently give an estimate for each card and explain it in terms of the reference group before counting to check. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how a known group helps when there are too many objects to count quickly. | Explain how to compare the unknown group with the reference. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils gave two plausible estimates with a reference-based reason. Offer a matching grid overlay to pupils who guessed without comparing.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-10 · Round to the Nearest Hundred

**Standards** · CS 1.6 · LS 1.6.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 34–35 · Activity Book pp. 22–23 (shared rounding reference)  
**Learning objective** · Round at least three of four whole numbers correctly to the nearest hundred and explain one decision using the midpoint.  
**Materials** · Open number lines, hundreds cards and mini-whiteboards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Place 417 between 400 and 500. Ask which hundred is closer. | Point to the nearer hundred and describe the distance. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model locating 249, 250 and 251 between 200 and 300. Mark the midpoint and model the convention of rounding halfway up. | Place each number on the line and explain whether it lies below, on or above the midpoint. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 349, 350, 680 and 951. Check at least three correct rounded values and one midpoint explanation. | Independently round all four numbers to the nearest hundred and show a number line for one. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how 951 can round to 1,000. | Identify 900 and 1,000 as the endpoints and explain that 951 is past 950. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils rounded at least three correctly and explained one. Revisit the midpoint with pupils who rounded 350 down.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-11 · Identify and Complete Number Patterns

**Standards** · CS 1.7 · LS 1.7.1, 1.7.2  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 36–39 · Activity Book pp. 24–26  
**Learning objective** · State the direction and step size of two patterns and fill at least three of four missing terms.  
**Materials** · Number cards, dry-erase sleeves and arrows labelled with step sizes.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show 355, 360, 365 and ask what changes between consecutive terms. | Say that each number increases by five. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model checking the same difference between more than one pair of terms. Show a decreasing pattern and one where a gap covers two steps. | Mark the step on arrows, explain the rule to a partner and fill missing positions on a card strip. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 246, 256, __, 276, __ and 740, 640, __, 440, __. Check both rules and the four missing terms. | Independently write the two rules and fill all four blanks. |
| Closure · 5 min | Show 425, 435, 445, 465 and ask which entry breaks the pattern. | Replace 465 with 455 and justify the correction using the step size. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils stated both rules and filled at least three blanks. Use labelled arrows to revisit direction or step size.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-12 · Solve Everyday Number Problems

**Standards** · CS 1.8 · LS 1.8.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 40–43 · Activity Book pp. 27–28  
**Learning objective** · Solve two everyday whole-number problems and show a suitable representation and a sentence answering each question.  
**Materials** · Task cards, place-value charts and blank number lines.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a locker clue with a 5 in hundreds and 0 in tens. Ask what extra information would identify its number. | Identify the missing ones digit. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model underlining useful facts in a short problem. Demonstrate a place-value chart for a digit clue and a number line for finding a missing value between two known values. | Choose a representation for each practice problem, solve it in pairs and explain how the solution fits the clues. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give two tasks: a delivery code with 6 hundreds, 0 tens and 8 ones, written in words, and a library total ten above 386 and ten below 406. Check the representation and both answers. | Solve both problems independently. Use a chart or number line and write one complete answer sentence per problem. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask pupils to check the library answer against both clues. | Show that 396 is ten above 386 and ten below 406. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils solved both problems and showed their reasoning. Give a short representation-matching task to pupils who used the wrong clues.
