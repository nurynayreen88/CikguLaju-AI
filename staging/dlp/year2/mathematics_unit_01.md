# Mathematics (DLP) · Year 2 SK · Unit 1 Numbers up to 1,000

These six CikguLaju lesson plans cover the selected number, writing, place-value and comparison standards in the KSSR (Semakan 2017) English-edition DSKP. Each plan is 60 minutes. The codes identify staging records. Source mapping and the remaining checks are recorded in the [DLP audit](../../../audits/dlp_sk_sources_20261114.md#year-2-batch-1-source-check).

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-01 · Recognise Numbers

**Standards** · CS 1.1 · LS 1.1.1(i), (ii), (iii)  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 2–5 · Activity Book pp. 1–2  
**Learning objective** · Say one number shown as a numeral and read one number shown in words, then match at least four of five numerals to their word forms.  
**Materials** · Number and word cards, including pairs such as 306 and 360.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show 306 and 360. Ask what changes when the zero moves. | Read the two numbers and compare what they hear. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model reading a three-digit number in place-value order. Give pairs shuffled numeral and word cards. | Match the cards. Read the written form and say the number on the numeral card to verify each match. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Show 520 and the words “seven hundred and two” as separate oral prompts. Check the five independent matches for 217, 260, 402, 420 and 618. | Refer to Activity Book pp. 1–2 if available. Match the five numbers to shuffled word cards independently and read both oral prompts to the teacher. |
| Closure · 5 min | Show 614 and 641 and ask which numeral matches “six hundred and fourteen”. | Choose a numeral and say it aloud. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils read both formats and matched at least four pairs. Practise numbers with a zero in tens or ones where needed.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-02 · Write Numbers

**Standards** · CS 1.2 · LS 1.2.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 6–7 · Activity Book pp. 3–4  
**Learning objective** · Write at least four of five numbers correctly, including one conversion in each direction between numerals and words.  
**Materials** · Mini-whiteboards, place-value mats and a five-item response slip.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Say “four hundred and seven”. Show 407 and 470 and ask which one matches. | Choose a numeral and explain what the zero represents. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use a hundreds-tens-ones mat to model 805, then show how to write it in words. Give pairs alternating numeral and word prompts. | Write each number in the other form and check a partner's response against the mat. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give “two hundred and eight” and “six hundred and thirty” for pupils to write in numerals, and 315, 470 and 906 to write in words. Note recurring zero or digit-order errors. | Refer to Activity Book pp. 3–4 if available. Complete the five conversions independently. |
| Closure · 5 min | Present one swapped-digit answer and ask pupils to correct it. | Write the correct answer and explain the change. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils scored at least four of five with success in both directions. Reteach the conversion direction that caused errors.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-03 · Show and Match Number Values

**Standards** · CS 1.1 · LS 1.1.2(i), (ii)  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 8–12 · Activity Book pp. 5–6  
**Learning objective** · Build two numbers using base-ten blocks and correctly match two block pictures to their numerals.  
**Materials** · Base-ten blocks, block pictures and numeral cards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a unit cube, a ten rod and a hundred flat. Ask what quantity each represents. | Name each quantity and explain how ten unit cubes form a ten. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model 284 with blocks. Provide a mixture of numeral cards and pictures, including numbers with zero tens or ones. | Build a number from a numeral, then switch roles and match a pictured group to its numeral. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask pupils to build 307 and 460. Show pictures of two flats, five rods and one cube, and of six flats and two cubes. Check the two builds and the matches to 251 and 602. | Refer to Activity Book pp. 5–6 if available. Build and match the four items independently. |
| Closure · 5 min | Show a group with four hundred flats and three unit cubes. | Hold up 403 and explain why no ten rods are needed. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils completed all four items. Rebuild a zero-place example with pupils who added blocks to fill an empty column.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-04 · Place Value and Digit Value

**Standards** · CS 1.4 · LS 1.4.1  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 13–15 · Activity Book pp. 7–9  
**Learning objective** · State both the place and digit value of a marked digit in at least three of four examples.  
**Materials** · Place-value mats, digit cards and mini-whiteboards.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show 242. Point to the two 2s and ask what each represents. | Describe the difference between the value of the first and last 2. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model “The place is tens. The digit value is 40.” Move a digit across columns and ask pupils to describe its new value. | Use a mat to change the digit positions, then give the place and digit value for a partner's marked digit. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Mark 3 in 318, 2 in 425, 9 in 619 and 0 in 708. Count an item correct when both the place and digit value are correct. | Refer to Activity Book pp. 7–9 if available. Write both answers for each marked digit independently. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask for the place and digit value of 0 in 503. | State “tens” and “zero” and point to the tens column. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils answered at least three items correctly. Use a place-value mat to distinguish the column name from the amount represented.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-05 · Partition Numbers

**Standards** · CS 1.4 · LS 1.4.2  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 16–17 · Activity Book pp. 10–11  
**Learning objective** · Partition two of three numbers correctly in both forms, using place names and expanded sums.  
**Materials** · Digit cards, place-value mats and response slips.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Display 300 + 50 + 8 and ask pupils to combine the parts. | Build 358 and name the three groups. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model 705 as seven hundreds, zero tens and five ones, then as 700 + 5. Use the mat to show why both forms represent the same number. | Partition three-digit numbers in both forms. Check each answer by recombining its parts. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 436, 802 and 570. Check that the values add to the original number and that the two forms agree. | Refer to Activity Book pp. 10–11 if available. Write both partitions for each number independently. |
| Closure · 5 min | Show 650 as 600 + 5 and ask pupils to repair the error. | Write 600 + 50 and explain the value of 5 in the tens place. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils wrote both forms for at least two numbers. Provide a place-value mat to pupils who used a digit in place of its value.

## DLP-MAT-Y2-U01-06 · Compare Numbers

**Standards** · CS 1.1 · LS 1.1.2(iii)  
**References** · Textbook Part 1 pp. 18–21 · Activity Book pp. 12–13  
**Learning objective** · Correctly compare at least three of four pairs of numbers up to 1,000 and explain one comparison using place value.  
**Materials** · Numeral cards, hundreds-tens-ones mats and a number line.

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show 391 and 319. Ask which number is greater and which column settles it. | Compare from the hundreds column and explain why the tens decide. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model comparing from the highest place. Include pairs with equal hundreds and equal tens. Extend the mat to thousands when comparing with 1,000 and check one comparison on a number line. | Arrange cards from each pair, tell a partner which number is greater and name the first place that differs. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give pairs 327 and 372, 608 and 604, 95 and 205, and 999 and 1,000. Check four independent comparisons and ask pupils to justify one using the deciding place. | Refer to Activity Book pp. 12–13 if available. Write four “greater than” or “less than” comparisons and explain one. |
| Closure · 5 min | Present 599 and 600 and ask what decides the comparison. | Choose 600 and explain that six hundreds exceed five hundreds. |

**Reflection** · ___ of ___ pupils compared at least three pairs and explained one. Revisit highest-place comparisons with pupils who relied on the ones digit.
