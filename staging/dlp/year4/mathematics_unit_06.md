## DLP-MAT-Y4-L57 · Lesson 57 · Identify and compare angles

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  5-9 Sep · B  6-10 Sep

**Duration** · 60 min

**Standards** · CS 6.1 · LS 6.1.1

**References** · Textbook pp. 199-200

**Learning objective** · Mark right, acute and obtuse angles on familiar two-dimensional shapes.

**Materials** · Cut-out triangles and quadrilaterals, paper right-angle tester

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a square corner and open it slightly wider. | Compare the opening with the right angle. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Use a corner tester to classify example angles as acute, right or obtuse. | Place the tester with its vertex on each marked angle and label the result. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give a rectangle and a printed obtuse triangle with marked vertices. | Identify right angles in the rectangle and one obtuse and two acute angles in the triangle. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask if turning a shape changes any angle sizes. | Rotate a cut-out and explain why the classification is the same. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Check tester alignment on an angle with slanted arms.

## DLP-MAT-Y4-L58 · Lesson 58 · Identify and draw parallel and perpendicular lines

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  5-9 Sep · B  6-10 Sep

**Duration** · 60 min

**Standards** · CS 6.2 · LS 6.2.1, 6.2.2

**References** · Textbook pp. 201-203

**Learning objective** · Label both kinds of lines in a diagram and draw a pair of each using a grid.

**Materials** · Grid paper, rulers and map drawings

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show two straight track lines and ask whether extending them would meet. | Identify parallel lines using the picture. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model identifying pairs that never meet and pairs crossing at a right angle. Draw examples on a grid. | Mark pairs in a grid map and draw one pair of each type. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Provide a grid with one vertical and two horizontal lines and ask pupils to label and draw two new pairs. | Correctly identify one parallel and one perpendicular pair and draw another example of each. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask how to check a right angle on a grid. | Use the horizontal and vertical grid directions. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Review the distinction between intersecting and perpendicular.

## DLP-MAT-Y4-L59 · Lesson 59 · Calculate polygon perimeters

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  12-15 Sep · B  13-15 Sep

**Duration** · 60 min

**Standards** · CS 6.3 · LS 6.3.1

**References** · Textbook pp. 204-205

**Learning objective** · Find a missing side length where needed and determine the perimeter of a polygon with up to eight sides.

**Materials** · Grid polygons and perimeter slips

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Trace a pentagon outline and ask which distances count toward perimeter. | Point to the full boundary. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model adding the side lengths. On an L-shape, find a missing horizontal length by comparing the total width with the known step. | Find an unknown side and sum all the sides on a practice grid shape. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give an L-shape with successive side lengths 8, 3, 3, 3, __ and 6 cm, starting from the bottom edge and moving anticlockwise. | Find the missing 5 cm by subtracting the 3 cm horizontal step from 8 cm, then calculate a perimeter of 28 cm. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask whether an internal grid line is part of the perimeter. | Trace the outer boundary and explain why the line is excluded. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Have pupils mark each boundary segment once before summing lengths.

## DLP-MAT-Y4-L60 · Lesson 60 · Calculate areas on squares and grids

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  12-15 Sep · B  13-15 Sep

**Duration** · 90 min

**Standards** · CS 6.3 · LS 6.3.2

**References** · Textbook pp. 206-208

**Learning objective** · Use a grid and a formula to calculate the areas of a rectangle and a triangle.

**Materials** · Square grid sheets, rectangle and triangle cut-outs

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 10 min | Show a 6-by-4 grid rectangle and ask how to count squares efficiently. | Count rows of four or columns of six. |
| Main activity · 45 min | Model area from the grid and pair two right triangles to make a rectangle. Show the base and perpendicular height on isosceles and equilateral examples. | Draw the rectangle and a right triangle, count or pair grid units, and compare with formulas. |
| Practice and assessment · 25 min | Use a 6 cm × 4 cm rectangle and a right triangle with base 6 cm and height 4 cm. | Find 24 cm² and 12 cm² and use two matching triangles to justify the second area. |
| Closure · 10 min | Ask why area uses square centimetres. | Link each grid square to one square centimetre. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Use a labelled base and perpendicular height to avoid choosing the slanted side.

## DLP-MAT-Y4-L61 · Lesson 61 · Calculate volumes of cubes and cuboids

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  19-23 Sep · B  20-24 Sep

**Duration** · 60 min

**Standards** · CS 6.4 · LS 6.4.1

**References** · Textbook pp. 209-210

**Learning objective** · Build a cuboid from unit cubes and show how layer counting gives the same result as the formula.

**Materials** · Unit cubes, layer mats, dimension cards

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show one layer with three rows of four cubes. | Count twelve cubes. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model repeating the same layer and label length, width and height of the resulting cuboid. | Build two layers and show that the multiplication and layer counts agree. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask for the volume of a 4 cm × 3 cm × 2 cm cuboid built from 1 cm cubes. | Find 24 cm³ and explain two layers of twelve cubes. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why volume uses cubic and not square units. | Point to a unit cube and identify all three dimensions. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Ask pupils to sketch layers before applying the formula.

## DLP-MAT-Y4-L62 · Lesson 62 · Solve a spatial measurement problem

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  19-23 Sep · B  20-24 Sep

**Duration** · 60 min

**Standards** · CS 6.5 · LS 6.5.1

**References** · Textbook pp. 211-213

**Learning objective** · Choose perimeter, area or volume and defend the choice using the units and the task context.

**Materials** · Fictional craft cards, grid and solid diagrams

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a question about putting ribbon around a rectangular card. | Choose perimeter as the relevant measure. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Contrast border length, surface covering and space filled using separate craft examples. | Match each question to its measure and compute one example with units. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask for ribbon around a 9 cm × 4 cm card and paper to cover one side. | Find 26 cm of ribbon and 36 cm² of paper, and explain which quantity uses which unit. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what would change if a box needed to be filled instead. | Identify volume and cubic units. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Review wording that distinguishes a boundary from a surface or space.

## DLP-MAT-Y4-R06 · Review 06 · Space Review

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  19-23 Sep · B  20-24 Sep

**Duration** · 60 min

**Standards** · Review LS 6.3.1, 6.3.2, 6.4.1

**References** · Textbook p. 214

**Learning objective** · Distinguish perimeter, area and volume and justify a solution using its units.

**Materials** · Three measurement diagrams, unit cards

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show a rectangle and ask which unit describes the edge. | Choose a length unit. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Review choosing perimeter, area or volume from context. Give one small diagram for each. | Match measures to tasks and calculate a selected one with units. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Ask for the border of a 4 cm × 3 cm card, its face area and the volume of a 2 cm cube. | Give 14 cm, 12 cm² and 8 cm³ and explain one choice. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask why numerical answers alone do not describe a measurement. | Use the different units to explain what was measured. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Revisit the context associated with the weakest choice.
