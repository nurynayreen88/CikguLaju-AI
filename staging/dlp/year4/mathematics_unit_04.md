Unit 4 Time

## DLP-MAT-Y4-L42 · Lesson 42 · Convert 12-hour and 24-hour times

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  11-15 Jul · B  12-16 Jul

**Duration** · 60 min

**Standards** · CS 4.1 · LS 4.1.1

**References** · Textbook pp. 139-142

**Learning objective** · Convert times in both directions, including one after noon and one close to midnight.

**Materials** · 12-hour and 24-hour clocks, time cards

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show 7:40 p.m. and ask when it occurs in the day. | Identify the evening. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model converting before and after noon, with 00:15 as an example just after midnight. | Match time cards in both formats, including 12 a.m. and 12 p.m. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 7:40 p.m. and 00:15 for opposite conversions. | Write 19:40 and 12:15 a.m., including the correct day period. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask for the 24-hour notation of 12 noon. | State 12:00 and distinguish it from midnight. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Practise using the hour from 00 to 12 correctly.

## DLP-MAT-Y4-L43 · Lesson 43 · Calculate duration within 24 hours

**Duration** · 60 min

**Standards** · CS 4.2 · LS 4.2.1

**References** · Textbook pp. 143-144

**Learning objective** · Draw a timeline and calculate an elapsed time in hours and minutes.

**Materials** · Open number lines, timetable slips

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Show 09:35 and 11:10 and ask how to count elapsed time. | Suggest jumping to the next full hour. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model a timeline with an initial jump to 10:00 and the remaining jumps. | Mark start and finish and label each duration jump. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Give 09:35 to 11:10 and check a correctly labelled timeline. | Show 25 minutes, one hour and ten minutes, then find 1 hour 35 minutes. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Practise counting over a full-hour boundary.

## DLP-MAT-Y4-L44 · Lesson 44 · Estimate and verify a duration

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  11-15 Jul · B  12-16 Jul

**Duration** · 60 min

**Standards** · CS 4.3 · LS 4.3.1

**References** · Textbook p. 145

**Learning objective** · Estimate a familiar activity's duration, measure it and compare the two values.

**Materials** · Stopwatch, pencil-sorting activity, record slips

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 5 min | Ask how long it may take to sort six pencils. | Make a timed estimate before starting. |
| Main activity · 30 min | Model consistent start and finish signals and ask pupils to record their estimates first. | Time a brief familiar sorting task and compare measured durations with estimates. |
| Practice and assessment · 20 min | Check each estimate, actual result and the numerical difference in seconds. | Submit both values with units and state whether the estimate was high or low. |
| Closure · 5 min | Ask what could improve the next estimate. | Use the measured duration as a reference. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Repeat with a different familiar short task and compare again.

## DLP-MAT-Y4-L45 · Lesson 45 · Relate and convert time units

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  18-22 Jul · B  19-23 Jul

**Duration** · 90 min

**Standards** · CS 4.4 · LS 4.4.1, 4.4.2

**References** · Textbook pp. 146-151

**Learning objective** · Explain relationships among longer units, then convert examples involving hours, days, weeks, months or years.

**Materials** · Calendar extracts, long-time-unit cards and response slips

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 10 min | Show a week on the calendar and ask how many days it contains. | Count seven days. |
| Main activity · 45 min | Build a conversion chart for hours and days, days and weeks, and months and years. Add 10 years per decade, 100 per century and 1,000 per millennium. | Use the chart to convert in both directions and explain how decades, centuries and a millennium are related. |
| Practice and assessment · 25 min | Ask for 3 days in hours, 4 weeks in days, 2 years in months and 2 centuries in years and decades. | Find 72 hours, 28 days, 24 months and 200 years or 20 decades. State how two centuries compare with one millennium. |
| Closure · 10 min | Ask whether every month has the same number of days. | Use a calendar to explain why month-to-day conversion needs a particular start and end date. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Review decades, centuries and millennium relationships using one number line.

## DLP-MAT-Y4-L46 · Lesson 46 · Add and subtract durations

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  18-22 Jul · B  19-23 Jul

**Duration** · 90 min

**Standards** · CS 4.5 · LS 4.5.1

**References** · Textbook pp. 152-157

**Learning objective** · Calculate elapsed totals and differences using at least two pairs of time units.

**Materials** · Duration strips, exchange cards, answer slips

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 10 min | Show 1 day 18 hours plus an activity of 11 hours. | Predict whether the hours exceed a full day. |
| Main activity · 45 min | Model exchanging 24 hours for one day. Demonstrate regrouping across one week when subtracting days. | Calculate totals and differences with units labelled at each step. |
| Practice and assessment · 25 min | Give 1 day 18 h + 11 h and 3 weeks 2 days − 1 week 5 days. | Find 2 days 5 h and 1 week 4 days, showing both exchanges. |
| Closure · 10 min | Ask how converting all quantities to days can check the second result. | Show that 23 − 12 gives 11 days. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Practise one exchange without treating time units as base ten.

## DLP-MAT-Y4-L47 · Lesson 47 · Multiply and divide durations

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  25-29 Jul · B  26-30 Jul

**Duration** · 90 min

**Standards** · CS 4.5 · LS 4.5.2

**References** · Textbook pp. 158-163

**Learning objective** · Scale a duration and find an equal share of another duration, with regrouping and units shown.

**Materials** · Timelines, duration cards, conversion slips

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 10 min | Show three blocks of 1 day 9 hours. | Estimate the combined duration in days. |
| Main activity · 45 min | Model scaling a duration after converting it to hours. Convert a separate weekly duration to days for equal sharing. | Calculate examples and regroup results into the requested units. |
| Practice and assessment · 25 min | Give 3 × (1 day 9 hours) and 3 weeks 4 days shared over five equal blocks. | Find 4 days 3 hours and 5 days per block, and show 99 hours and 25 days in the working. |
| Closure · 10 min | Ask how multiplying five blocks of five days checks the second result. | Recover 25 days or 3 weeks 4 days. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Revisit converting a duration to a single unit before sharing it.

## DLP-MAT-Y4-L48 · Lesson 48 · Solve a timetable problem

**RPT 2027 dates** · A  25-29 Jul · B  26-30 Jul

**Duration** · 90 min

**Standards** · CS 4.6 · LS 4.6.1

**References** · Textbook pp. 164-167

**Learning objective** · Use a timetable to determine start, finish or elapsed time and check the answer for overlaps.

**Materials** · Fictional timetable, blank timeline cards

| Phase | Teacher | Pupils |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set induction · 10 min | Show consecutive activities ending at 10:15 and starting at 10:10. | Identify the overlap. |
| Main activity · 45 min | Model placing start and finish times on a timeline and checking whether two events meet or overlap. | Compare timetable entries and adjust one start time to avoid a clash. |
| Practice and assessment · 25 min | Give a 09:20 start and duration 55 minutes, with another event at 10:10. | Find a 10:15 finish and five-minute overlap and suggest 10:15 as the earliest next start. |
| Closure · 10 min | Ask why subtracting times gives the overlap duration. | Point to the common interval and explain. |

**Reflection** · ___ / ___ pupils met the objective. Recheck the start times before redrawing an unfamiliar timetable.
